RPU represents estimated manufacturer revenue per vehicle, not MSRP, dealer asking price, or dealer gross profit. Model-level realized wholesale prices are not directly established in the research. Estimates calibrated to regional revenue remain estimates.

The workbook already implements:

**Model net RPU = reference net RPU × (1 + cumulative price change versus reference) − (current brand incentive cost per vehicle − reference brand incentive cost per vehicle).**

Reference RPU is already net of baseline incentives. Subtract only the change in support, not the entire incentive a second time. Dealer-funded discounts are not automatically an expense to Stellantis. Financing and lease support should be captured consistently with the chosen incentive measure; advertised cash rebates alone are not necessarily total support.

2. **Cumulative price changes.** Repeated model rows such as 12, 23, 57 and 68. These are cumulative versus the fixed reference, not annual YoY growth. Specify how FX and within-model mix are treated so they are not hidden or counted twice.
3. **Brand manufacturer incentive cost per vehicle.** `Model Assumptions` rows 7–11: Ram, Jeep, Chrysler, Dodge, Other. Populate both reference and forecast periods. Brand averages applied to individual models are an explicit simplification; explain any material model divergence.
4. **Canada and Mexico net RPU.** `NA RPU Build` rows 226–227. Keep aggregate assumptions, already net of incentives.
5. **Other regional revenue/reconciliation.** Row 247. Historical calibration may identify a residual but does not prove it is parts/service revenue. Choose and explain its forecast treatment.
