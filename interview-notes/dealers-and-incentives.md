# Dealers and incentives: ordering, clearance and replenishment

Updated 19 October 2026. Recap of existing operator research plus a bounded refresh of Cox September inventory and Stellantis Q3 shipment release. No forecast changes.

## Dealer decision in plain language

A dealer wants vehicles it can sell at an attractive expected margin within a manageable holding period. Relevant signals include local model/trim sales, inventory age, incoming orders, customer inquiries and conversion, competitor offers, payment affordability, earned OEM support and available floorplan capacity. This is an operator framework, not a verified formula used identically by all dealers. Allocation and order restrictions limit actual choice.

Expected economics: selling proceeds minus acquisition cost plus earned support minus holding and selling costs. Financing/insurance income, trade-in opportunities and future service add potential value, but are not guaranteed and are not identical to new-vehicle gross profit.

## What went wrong and what improved

- Scarcity had supported strong vehicle margins; restored supply brought competition while affordability remained strained.
- Wrong configurations and high-priced stock could accumulate even when desired trucks were unavailable.
- Aged stock required markdowns and tied up financing capacity. Discounts are paid by the OEM, dealer or both; advertised savings alone cannot identify incidence.
- Asbury January 2025 call described Q4 clearance of overpriced/undesirable inventory at poor gross profit, while ordering restrictions on some desired options were easing.
- Asbury reported Stellantis units +3% in Q1 2025 and +15.6% in Q2, but did not supply matching brand-specific inventory and gross profit. Lithia still described weakness in July 2025. Improvement was uneven.
- Ram sales-linked bonuses reported effective February 2025 provide a concrete mechanism: eligible payments at target thresholds, retroactive to the first unit. Exact current rules and earned payouts remain unknown. Do not describe retail bonuses as invented in 2025 or a proven universal replacement of wholesale incentives.

## Incentive distinctions for the next deep dive

- Customer rebate: reduces buyer outlay if eligible; OEM pays the support.
- Subsidized loan/lease: changes payment through rates, credits or residual support; compare complete terms, not just headline monthly payment.
- Dealer cash/coupons: reduces effective dealer economics subject to eligibility; pass-through is not necessarily complete.
- Sales-target bonus: rewards qualifying sell-through; threshold/retroactivity can strongly affect the marginal deal, but expected payout depends on attainability.
- Dealer markdown: directly surrenders dealer margin unless offset by support.

Question to answer: does support make fresh-stock sales and repeat orders profitable, or merely clear aged stock? Need matched trim/ZIP/month offers, invoice/support terms, stock age, actual sales and earned dealer margin. A rising incentive alone cannot distinguish weak demand from a deliberate competitive-price reset.

## Model implication and provenance

Positive dealer engagement supports a possible recovery mechanism; elevated stock still challenges a rapid support taper and sustained wholesale outperformance of customer sales. Do not mechanically change assumptions from one inventory snapshot. Require repeated fresh-stock sell-through and reorders.
