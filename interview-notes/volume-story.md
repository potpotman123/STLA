# Volume story: model-by-model sales, availability and price/mix

Updated 19 October 2026. Expands and renames the Ram interview recap; VCP notes remain separate and unchanged. No numerical forecast changes or spreadsheet edits.

US customer sales include retail and fleet; they are not North American wholesale shipments. Ram 1500 is light-duty; 2500/3500 are heavy-duty. Grand Cherokee is a different product from the smaller Cherokee.

## 1. Ram 1500: launch disruption → availability → sales and mix

## Correct the timeline

- Fifth-generation DT was introduced for model year 2019, in calendar 2018. The disruption under discussion is the substantial MY2025 update/changeover during calendar 2024, not the original fifth-generation launch.
- MY2025 brought Hurricane engines and equipment/electronics changes. Do not call it a completely new sixth-generation truck.
- Sources: [2019 launch](https://www.media.stellantis.com/me-en/ram-archive/press/new-2019-ram-1500-at-the-2018-geneva-international-motor-show), [MY2025 OEM product announcement](https://blog.stellantisnorthamerica.com/2023/12/02/new-2025-ram-1500-with-more-powerful-more-fuel-efficient-hurricane-engine-family-unveiled/).

### What is directly supported

[Kuniskis interview, WardsAuto, 14 January 2025](https://www.wardsauto.com/news/fixing-ram-brand-starts-at-the-top/799058/): management attributed the difficult changeover to simultaneous exterior, ADAS, electrical architecture/connectors/wiring/modules and powertrain changes. It reported missed production ramp/daily output and inadequate trim mix. At the time, it cited roughly two weeks' Tungsten supply and 40 days' Rebel supply across 2,600 dealers. This is management testimony, not an independent engineering fault tree; it does not establish which specific connector/module caused which delay.

### Explain the economics plainly

1. Changing several interdependent systems during a live factory changeover creates validation, sequencing and ramp risk. The precise component-level bottleneck remains unverified.
2. Missing planned output and trim mix means dealers cannot reliably offer the particular truck a shopper wants. Buyers can wait, accept another configuration, buy used or choose a competing brand. We have not measured each destination.
3. Total dealer stock can be high while the desired trim/engine combination is scarce. Unsold old or mismatched stock is not a substitute for every missing new truck.
4. Weak conversion on existing inventory can require discounts and reduce dealer reordering; missing new production can directly constrain shipments. These channels can coexist, with different timing.
5. Manufacturer revenue responds to shipments and net revenue per shipment, not just consumer sales. Missing expensive trims can hurt mix; old-stock incentives can hurt net price. Exact contributions are not isolated.

### Why this was not just a factory problem

The prior dossier also identifies Classic wind-down/value-offering loss, HEMI withdrawal and buyer preferences, and affordability pressure. Launch stabilization alone does not repair every cause. No evidence establishes the pandemic chip shortage as the cause of this later changeover failure. See [Ram dossier](../research/model-diligence-2026-10-14/ram-diligence.md).

### Recovery thesis for the model

Better availability and configuration choice + restored engine/entry choices + competitive purchase terms can recover sales that were constrained or diverted. Upper-trim availability can improve mix, while entry/fleet growth and incentives can offset that benefit. Do not assume volume recovery means recovered pricing power, that every lost sale returns, or that all subsequent growth is retail conquest. Early retail recovery preceded HEMI's later return, so HEMI cannot be the sole mechanism.

### Interview sentence

“Ram's problem was partly an availability-and-choice mismatch: a difficult MY2025 changeover prevented the factory from delivering the intended volume and trim mix, while affordability and engine choices also weakened the proposition. Dealers could therefore have too much of some trucks and too few of the trucks particular customers wanted. My recovery thesis is improved execution and a repaired offering, with continued incentive support—not automatic restoration of an old sales peak.”


## 2. Grand Cherokee: a less competitive offer, changing powertrain mix, then production adjustments

### Was there a healthy franchise beforehand?

Observed family US sales: 2019 **242,969**; 2021 **264,444**; 2023 **244,594**; 2024 **216,148**; 2025 **210,082**. This is not a continuous collapse since before the pandemic. However, the new three-row L, old/new generation overlap and subsequent 4xe broadened the family, so stable aggregate sales do not establish unchanged demand for the same product. In 2023, 45,684 sales were 4xe; gasoline/non-4xe sales were 198,910. This is mix arithmetic, not evidence that every missing gasoline buyer left Jeep.

### Concrete mechanisms and the limits of the evidence

- **A higher price for much the same product:** MY2023 new-generation Laredo rose $2,710 to $42,830 including destination; Limited rose $3,210. The Laredo change is approximately 6.8%. This increases financing burden absent offsetting incentives. Posted MSRP is not an observed monthly payment or transaction price.
- **Desired capability tied to an expensive powertrain:** Trailhawk became 4xe-only, starting at $67,055. A customer seeking that off-road configuration now had to accept a plug-in hybrid package or choose another trim/product. The mechanism is restricted choice and a higher purchase hurdle, not proof of how many buyers defected. [Contemporaneous prices and specifications](https://www.cars.com/articles/jeep-grand-cherokee-which-should-you-buy-2022-or-2023-459837/).
- **Recovery tools differed by powertrain:** January 2024 gasoline MSRP cuts of $2,000–$4,000 excluded 4xe. Some rebates also changed, so the sticker cut cannot simply be treated as an equal net-payment reduction. [Dated pricing report](https://www.carsdirect.com/automotive-news/2024-jeep-grand-cherokee-prices-cut-by-up-to-4-000).
- **Financing can change the ranking of alternatives:** prior dated forum/lender evidence showed a quoted CCAP PHEV lease credit declining from $7,500 to $3,750 in spring 2024, while another lender reportedly retained $7,500. This supports a possible payment disadvantage, but is not a matched national offer panel. It does not identify the causal sales effect.
- **Reliability concerns can weaken willingness to buy:** recalls and buyer accounts support a plausible confidence mechanism. The October 2024 recall cannot explain the earlier Q2 onset on its own; forum anecdotes cannot establish prevalence.
- **Inventory and production feed back on each other:** excess stock can trigger production cuts. A later fall in factory output therefore need not mean demand was healthy but supply prevented sales. Conversely, planned downtime can temporarily constrain a particular configuration.

### The crucial split: the cause cannot be constant throughout 2024

Derived from published family and 4xe counts; non-4xe is the residual:

| Change versus same half of 2023 | H1 2024 | H2 2024 |
|---|---:|---:|
| 4xe | +1,137 | −19,231 |
| Non-4xe | −19,342 | +8,990 |
| Total family | −18,205 | −10,241 |

The early loss was concentrated outside 4xe; later gasoline/non-4xe recovered while 4xe fell 69%. Across the full year, 4xe accounted arithmetically for 18,094 of 28,446 lost units (64%). That does **not** mean lease support caused 64% of the decline. The evidence is consistent with affordability/powertrain substitution and channel changes; we do not have a causal allocation among them. Q4 reported retail improvement alongside a total-sales decline also warns against treating every lost fleet sale as lost household demand.

### What this means going forward

- A production restart alone is insufficient. We need competitive effective payments, available desirable trims and evidence that customers displaced from 4xe remain within the family.
- The 2026 refresh changes the offer again. Treat product improvements as recovery mechanisms to test, not guaranteed recovered units.
- H1 2026 family sales of 101,737 imply 203,474 annualized, but Q3 was 38,027. Management discussed inventory built ahead of summer downtime and more H1-weighted rental sales; neither statement provides a GC-specific retail/fleet allocation. Management attributed roughly 25,000 fewer NA GC shipments in Q3 primarily to planned downtime. Do not equate this with the US customer-sales decline: population and measure differ.
- Existing 2028 **185,000 net units** is a conditional forecast, not a proven steady state. Require post-downtime retail sell-through, sustainable dealer reorder behavior and stable effective pricing before raising it. Dealer replenishment and rental timing alone would be weak evidence.
- Price/mix implication: replacing expensive 4xe volume with cheaper gasoline trims could improve units while lowering mix; reduced discounting could offset that. Do not infer RPU growth automatically from volume recovery.

**Interview sentence:** “Grand Cherokee had a real customer franchise, but Jeep increased the price hurdle and changed the combinations of capability and powertrain customers could buy. The 2024 decline then changed character: gasoline models drove the first-half loss, whereas 4xe drove the second-half loss even as other variants recovered. That makes this a product-value and powertrain-mix recovery case, with production timing layered on top—not simply Ram's launch problem repeated.”
