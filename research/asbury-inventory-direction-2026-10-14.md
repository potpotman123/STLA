# Asbury: inventory replacement versus restocking

Decision: whether to assume Stellantis shipments grow faster than customer sales through dealer inventory accumulation. Reviewed 14 October 2026; no workbook assumptions changed.

## New evidence

- **28 April 2026 call, Dan Clara, Q&A:** Stellantis remained the largest domestic margin headwind. He supported management's pricing changes but explained that cheaper incoming models undermine the pricing of older stock, requiring clearance at pressured margins. [Transcript reproduction](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/04/28/asbury-abg-q1-2026-earnings-call-transcript/), questions from Barclays and Jefferies.
- **28 July 2026 call, Dan Clara, first Q&A:** inventory mix at Stellantis stores was starting to improve, but replacing expensive older inventory takes time. [Transcript reproduction](https://earningswhispers.com/transcript/ABG/Q22026). The transcript also reports a 28% decline at Stellantis stores, but its comparison period is ambiguously transcribed. Do not enter that percentage into the model without verifying the recording or a reliable independent transcript.

These are management statements reproduced by third parties, not independent retail measurements. July's transcription contains errors. Neither passage quantifies Stellantis-specific orders, inventory aging, cancellation rates or dealer inventory targets. Group-wide inventory days and used-car purchasing comments must not be treated as Stellantis new-car data.

## Interpretation and model treatment

The balance of these passages favors continuing cleanup with selective replacement, rather than broad enthusiasm for aggregate inventory expansion. Support for better-priced incoming products and pressure from legacy stock coexist. This qualifies the positive 2025 Asbury commentary previously used in the recovery case; it does not establish a deterioration across the entire dealer network.

Mechanism inferred: lower-priced new vehicles make older, higher-cost vehicles less competitive; clearing the older vehicles pressures dealer margins and occupies capital and lot space until sale. Better products can support future throughput without requiring more total dealer inventory.

Do not add an independent restocking uplift solely from improving management tone. Start from customer-sales-driven shipments, with inventory movements supported separately. Exact wholesale/customer-sales ratios remain unselected and require aligned geography, fleet treatment, timing and inventory scope. Asbury alone cannot determine the number of units to destock or a 2028 equilibrium inventory level.

Evidence that would justify upgrading: declining aged inventory alongside improving Stellantis-specific margins and repeat replenishment orders for new products. Falling inventory alone can also reflect restricted supply and is insufficient.
