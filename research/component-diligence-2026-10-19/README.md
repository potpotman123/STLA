# Component VCP diligence: what can actually become cheaper?

19 October 2026. Investment research, not an approved forecast change. No financial workbook opened or edited. Current forecast inputs are used only as weighting references; no volume, RPU or VCP assumption changed.

## Decision first

A meaningful component-cost opportunity is credible. Public evidence does **not** yet substantiate the full €3.15bn annual North American component saving embedded in our 9% scenario. The evidence supports identifiable mechanisms, not a complete Stellantis project inventory with prices, validation costs and 2028 adoption. This is neither proof that the opportunity is zero nor a quantified upper bound.

The strongest new company-specific lead is a **~70% cost reduction target for the L2+ hands-free-highway assistance system**, with extended features in 2027. It is a small, defined system, not all electronics or all vehicle cost. The most transparent dollar example is Aptiv's **$40 electrical-center redesign** for an unnamed OEM. For large BEV batteries, national-lab models support much larger dollar pathways, but their relevance depends on actual propulsion mix and contracts. The gasoline-heavy portfolio cannot inherit those savings automatically.

The important correction to our earlier chat: Investor Day identifies four cost workstreams—components, manufacturing, quality, logistics/indirect. Quality is not merely our add-on. The three-driver shorthand was incomplete. Management's 75% is new-vehicle cost, and 80% is planned component review coverage, not realized saving or fitment. [Finance transcript p2](https://www.stellantis.com/content/dam/stellantis-corporate/investors/events/stellantis-investor-day-2026/transcript/Stellantis-Investor-Day-2026-FaSTLAne2030-Transcript-Financial-Overview.pdf)

## What an OEM component price contains

The invoice can recover raw materials, purchased subcomponents, processing/assembly, scrap, supplier engineering/tooling, overhead, profit and delivery. Terms differ; never invent a standard percentage split. A part is usually variable with production for Stellantis but contains fixed and step-fixed costs at its supplier. Minimum-volume, tooling and cancellation obligations weaken short-run variability. If a supplier improves utilization, Stellantis benefits only through price or commercial terms—not automatically.

A competent engineer asks what **function** must be preserved before asking what part can disappear. Safety/emissions are performance constraints; they do not make a part's price immutable. Nor does a cheaper material necessarily produce a cheaper qualified assembly.

Accounting boundaries:
- Purchased module productivity belongs to components if captured in the OEM purchase price. Do not count the same supplier labor saving as Stellantis plant labor.
- Internal engine machining/assembly labor belongs to manufacturing. Hurricane is produced at Saltillo North; treating a hypothetical complete-engine invoice as external parts while also counting internal labor would overlap.
- Paint chemicals are consumables; booth labor/energy and application losses require a consistent manufacturing allocation.
- Shipping is counted once, whether embedded in price or in our logistics schedule. Tariffs and commodities are separate offsets, not design productivity.
- OEM development/tooling cash investment is separate from recurring price saving. Some engineering is expensed; some development/tooling is capitalized with later amortization/depreciation. Do not subtract all capex as current AOI expense.
- Cheaper purchased parts initially affect inventory. Profit generally benefits as corresponding vehicles are sold. Supplier warranty in a price is not the OEM's entire warranty provision.

## Cost evidence and strongest case studies

### 1. Electrical distribution: a real removal of duplication, small per-vehicle dollars

Aptiv demonstrated replacing three conventional electrical centers with one smart and one conventional center. It reports $40 and2kg reductions; affected wiring is included. The price year, tooling and supplier invoice change are unknown. Product-family production since 2019 does not prove this exact demonstration entered Stellantis production. [Original case, pp1–2](https://www.aptiv.com/docs/default-source/case-studies/aptiv-case-study-smart-electrical-centers.pdf?sfvrsn=dbc9c73d_10)

Economic reasoning: electronic switching can remove duplicated boxes, fuses, relays and cable routing. New electronics, software and fault protection still cost money. Current electrical architecture, supplier award and validation determine whether this is a running change or a larger redesign. Do not add a separate harness reduction already included in the $40, or add a full-zonal-architecture target on top of it.

This is a useful mechanism proof, not a large enough standalone explanation of our target. At an explicitly hypothetical465,000eligible USRam LD+Grand Cherokee units in 2028, unchanged $40 would mean $18.6m before undisclosed implementation costs. Actual fitment remains unknown. We retainUSD and do not insert this undated price into the 2025EUR cost base.

### 2. Stellantis L2+: the best company-specific target to interrogate

The technology presentation targets~70% lower L2+ system cost. STLA Brain is planned forUS 2028; SmartCockpit is described for Grand Cherokee NA 2027; the 2028 door-to-door supervised system is separately described. None of these statements establishes a dollar per Ram savings bridge. TheL2+ target is not a 70% cut to airbags, braking, infotainment or allcontrollers. [Official technology deck, slides18/21/23](https://www.stellantis.com/content/dam/stellantis-corporate/investors/events/stellantis-investor-day-2026/presentation/Stellantis-Investor-Day-2026-FaSTLAne2030-Product-and-Technology.pdf)

For baseline comparableL2+system cost B and eligible equipped sold units N, target saving=0.70×B×N. Every100currencyunits ofB across100,000vehicles produces7m of thatcurrency. This is a scale ruler, not a chosenB. Expansion to previously unequipped vehicles adds system cost; compare to each vehicle's real baseline. Software/licensing, new compute and integration must be included. Shared compute savings overlap Brain/cockpit targets.

Why it may not already be optimized: management's dated architecture rollout supports a timing constraint. It does not by itself establish prior incompetence. Disconfirm by finding the claimed low-cost hardware already in the 2025baseline or by showing added features/licensing absorb the reduction.

### 3. Seating: a production mechanism with a narrow denominator

Adient states modular assembly cuts direct front-row labor at its just-in-time assembly operation by more than 30%, with production inFY24. Modules arrive more complete from upstream operations. The result is not 30% of the seat invoice: upstream labor, freight and the share passed through to the OEM are unresolved. [SEC-filed presentation, slide7](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1670541/000167054123000163/adient-barclaysglobalmob.htm)

A seat includes a frame, recliner/track, foam, upholstery, electronics and comfort equipment. Hidden module commonality can preserve appearance and comfort; deleting ventilation or replacing upholstery changes customer content. The cost model needs net whole supply chain labor saving, not just a cheaper final workstation. Existing production elsewhere also means this technique may already be inside a supplier's quote.

### 5. Engine and transmission: newer is not necessarily cheaper

EPA/FEV estimated that replacing a5.4LV8 with a turbocharged direct-injection3.5LV6 added $846.26, while one six-speedAT cost $105.53less than its five-speed predecessor due to a different gear arrangement. Both are 2008/09-cost engineering comparisons at450,000annualunits, not currentRamquotes. [Executive summary and§1.3](https://nepis.epa.gov/Exe/ZyPURL.cgi?Dockey=P100GDM2.TXT)

The lesson is physical: fewer cylinders save some mechanical pieces but add boost, fuel and cooling equipment; more gear ratios can sometimes use a simpler mechanical arrangement. Neither cylinder count nor gear count is a reliable cost proxy. A current8HP75 pickup transmission cannot be replaced with that oldCamry design while claiming equivalent towing/performance. No credit assigned.

Hurricane already incorporates advanced block/rotating/thermal technologies and in-house production. Do not count those existing features as future engineering discoveries. [OEM engine description](https://blog.stellantisnorthamerica.com/2022/03/25/stellantis-debuts-hurricane-twin-turbo-i-6-engine-that-cuts-emissions-increases-fuel-economy-and-is-more-powerful/)

### 6. Body/materials: weight savings can cost money

Ram already advertised a 98% high-strength steel frame and100lbframe reduction for the 2019DTgeneration. That proves prior optimization activity, not that further gains are exhausted. DOE lightweighting targets have explicitly allowed positive cost per pound removed. A giant casting, aluminum substitution or composite is not automatically a purchasing saving after tooling, scrap, joining, repair and lifecycle volume. Do not translate a weight percentage into a cost percentage. [Ram brochure](https://www.ramtrucks.com/content/dam/fca-brands/na/ramtrucks/en_us/global/pdf/brochure/19MY%20Ram%201500%20DT%20eBrochure.pdf), [DOE cost/weight target](https://www.energy.gov/sites/prod/files/2020/04/f73/doe-fy2021-budget-volume-3-part-1.pdf)

## Historical analogues: what transfers and what does not

Nissan reported a 9% purchasing reduction in FY2001 within its broader 20% program, with JPY245bn net accounting benefit. Product enrichment and regulation separately added JPY62bn of cost. This purchasing outcome combined commercial, organizational and technical activity. The source does not disclose a matching procurement denominator or an engineering-only share. Do not reverse-engineer a purchase base from net profit benefit divided by 9%. [Nissan FY2001 review](https://www.nissan-global.com/EN/DOCUMENT/HTML/FINANCIAL/SPEECH/2002/fs_speech20020509.html)

Toyota's TNGA 20% result concerns development resources, not a 20% recurring materials-bill reduction. Sharing and bulk purchasing are relevant mechanisms; the percentage has the wrong denominator for our component pool. [Toyota 2016 update](https://global.toyota/en/detail/14384838)

Stellantis had already reported €8.4bn of 2023 net cash synergies across purchasing, platforms, SG&A and other functions. This broad cash metric cannot be subtracted from component VCP; it prevents treating group purchasing and platform sharing as newly invented levers. [Annual report p24](https://www.stellantis.com/content/dam/stellantis-corporate/investors/financial-reports/Stellantis-NV-20231231-Annual-Report.pdf)

## Reconciliation to €35bn and 9%

Our €35bn estimated 2025 North American component pool was allocated from a disclosed global purchasing floor above €88bn. It is not a reported regional expense. With 1.472m reference shipments, it implies €23,777 of purchases per reference vehicle. A 9% reduction means **€2,140 per reference vehicle**, or €3.15bn of terminal annual benefit.

Dividing that €3.15bn by our proposed 1.6181m shipments in 2028 gives €1,947 per vehicle as a scale comparison. This does not rebase the frozen cost pool or authorize volume-scaling the saving.

The allocation implicitly assumes roughly 1.8 times North American component intensity versus the rest of the group, treating the €88bn floor as a point estimate. Model-specific bills of materials have not validated that ratio. Purchases versus consumption, inventory, service parts, geography and internal production still require reconciliation. [Existing cost-base derivation](../investor-day-2026-05-21/vcp-global-and-na-bases.md)

If actual implemented spend coverage is 80%, 50% or 35%, the reduction needed on that spend to achieve 9% of the whole pool is 11.25%, 18% or 25.7%, respectively. Those are algebraic sensitivities. Management's review coverage is not implemented coverage. Three equal annual reductions require 3.095% each; two cycles require 4.606% each. These calculations clarify the burden of proof rather than validating supplier concessions.

The undated USD40 example and constant-2023-USD battery figures are deliberately **not summed into a 2025-EUR estimate**. FX alone cannot repair price-year, scope or adoption differences. Calculators preserve source currencies and leave EUR forecast credit blank. Comparable quotations or suitable cost-category normalization would be required.

## Ranking by potential decision impact

1. **Purchasing and supplier engineering on existing products:** broadest potential exposure and earliest impact. Need signed prices and project registers; ordinary annual productivity must be included within 9%, not added again.
2. **Electronic architecture and L2+:** a specific company target and dates. Missing system dollars, trim fitment, software/license costs and overlap make this the best targeted follow-up.
3. **Batteries and packs:** potentially large dollars per BEV, but narrower regional exposure and substantial integration constraints. Do not transfer BEV cost learning to the ICE or HEV pool.
4. **Powertrain and body redesign:** large systems, constrained by committed tooling, validation and product cycles. Ideas without named programs remain technical possibilities.
5. **Seats and modular assemblies:** plausible productivity, but the narrow labor denominator and upstream work must be reconciled.
6. **Electrical centers and occupant detection:** tangible examples that could scale, but insufficient individually to explain several thousand euros per vehicle.

## Model admission and investment conclusion

Do not change the 9% forecast automatically. Label its evidence accurately: **an ambitious aggregate benchmark scenario, not a validated project build**. This work supports real mechanisms and identifies why adding their headline percentages would misstate the opportunity.

The bull case requires many current-product projects reaching production, contractually captured savings and broad eligible exposure. Ordinary productivity and commodity offsets must already be reconciled. New-platform announcements alone are insufficient for 2028 earnings.

The bull case would weaken if major programs slip beyond 2028; reviewed spending is already optimized; new features or licensing absorb hardware savings; supplier compensation offsets concessions; remaining program volumes cannot justify tooling; warranty severity rises after cheaper designs; or gross targets include savings already in our baseline.

It would strengthen with dated supplier awards or cost-engineering registers showing the original and replacement specification, net invoice price, approval, eligible models, production date, investment and annual sold units. A focused procurement/engineering interview on those fields would add more value than further generic cost-cutting commentary.

The public build covers 24 system families and several quantified comparators, but has **no verified project combining a matched Stellantis EUR unit saving with 2028 North American coverage**. The €3.15bn remains unsubstantiated by a complete project build. That is not disproof, a zero estimate or an upper bound. No paid data, bulk scraping, large OCR job or large agent deployment was used; two focused research tracks supplemented existing work.
