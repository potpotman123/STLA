# Multisystem engineering savings: correction to the $40 framing

19 October 2026. Research addendum; no spreadsheet or forecast changes.

The previous answer confused the absence of confirmed Stellantis adoption with the absence of a quantifiable engineering opportunity. A $40 electrical-center project is neither an electrical-system ceiling nor a vehicle-wide estimate.

## New comparator evidence

- [McKinsey/Caresoft teardown research, September 2026](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/automotive-and-assembly/our-insights/building-profitable-evs-ten-structural-design-moves-to-cut-material-costs): estimates 10–25% EV material-cost opportunity. Midsize-SUV battery/motor/power-electronics technology package: $400–1,000 per vehicle. Seat content changes: about $250. Variant simplification: $150–400, including nonmaterial costs. Pickup efficiency/battery-sizing comparison: about $2,000, a separate comparator. Requirements review: $120–240, potentially overlapping powertrain changes. These are engineering benchmarks, not confirmed Stellantis savings.
- [WorldAutoSteel/Ricardo report, May 2026](https://www.worldautosteel.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/WorldAutoSteel-A4-PC-Study_512.pdf): virtual front-body redesign reduces piece cost 10%, parts 38 to 25. This percentage applies to the front structure, not the entire body. Capex avoided is separate. Figure 2 shows one strut-top design at $37.30 versus $26.20 excluding assembly; narrative uses a different approximate percentage. Use the stated cost boundary rather than mixing those percentages.
- [EFESO thermal-management case](https://www.efeso.com/client-results/product-cost-optimization-for-automotive-thermal-management-system/): >10% material/manufacturing savings indicated on a supplier project with one year to production; redesign, sourcing and process work. Neither exclusively materials nor verified OEM invoice pass-through.
- [Aptiv 2020 sustainability report, p22](https://downloads.aptiv.com/libraries/docs/default-source/aptiv-storage/2020_aptiv_sustainability_report_f.pdf?sfvrsn=f2ae913e_2): satellite ADAS compute offers 10% like-for-like cost savings. Applies to that system, not all electronics.

## Aggregation and model decision

Conditional cross-case arithmetic: $400–1,000 powertrain package + $250 seat changes = $650–1,250 per eligible BEV. This combines distinct case examples; it is NOT an observed package on one vehicle. Seat changes remove features, so this is not a constant-content efficiency estimate. Adding variant simplification gives $800–1,650 across mixed cost categories, not exclusively component savings. Exclude both from the workbook until eligibility and accounting boundaries are established.

Do not add the $2,000 battery-sizing case, chemistry-alone savings, requirements review and the composite powertrain package: shared mechanisms and incompatible baselines can exaggerate savings. Do not add electrical-center savings to an electronics estimate already including that function.

A genuinely sourced whole-vehicle engineering range is the 10–25% EV benchmark; translating it into currency requires a measured eligible material baseline. Each $10,000 of that baseline implies $1,000–2,500 of potential, before rollout and implementation costs. This is arithmetic, not an assumed Ram bill of materials.

For our NA component forecast, retain the existing assumption pending adoption evidence. The benchmark makes a substantial engineering opportunity credible but does not validate 9% across an ICE-heavy portfolio by 2028. Need vehicle/platform eligibility, incremental changes versus already-integrated systems, supplier pass-through, launch timing, tooling/engineering costs and any feature-content effect. No matched current-dollar ICE engine/transmission, chassis/brake or whole-interior savings estimate was established in this bounded follow-up. Those unknowns are not zero savings.

Public engineering evidence can calibrate project potential; it cannot supply confidential Stellantis supplier invoices. The next useful route is matched incumbent-versus-new platform teardown data, rather than more small isolated savings anecdotes.
