# Stellantis US vehicle forecast audit

As of 14 October 2026. D098. Units are US customer deliveries, retail plus fleet, in thousands unless specified. The prior research forecast is preserved in baseline/. No workbook changed.

The audit removes unsupported growth coefficients, makes launch scenarios explicit and leaves unresolved substitution visible. This improves traceability; it does not establish precisely identified 2028 consumer demand. The central values below are conditional planning cases. Current sales do not independently validate future demand.

## What changed

- Chrysler 2028 timing has stronger primary support than the previous answer acknowledged: management explicitly connected 2028 with Chrysler launch and sales maturation [N01]. Exact individual dates remain unknown.
- Ram HD is not uniquely weak: GM combined HD sales fell 8.8% in Q3 and 11.1% YTD, versus Ram−6.0%/+3.4%. This changes causal attribution, not automatically Ram’s forecast [N07].
- GM plans refreshed full-size pickups for Q4 2026, a specific competitive challenge to retaining Ram LD gains [N08].
- The Pacifica update was planned for summer 2026 despite its 2027 model year. Do not book it again as a new calendar 2027 event [N16].
- Current source data support levels better than growth slopes. Ram LD 285 k, GC 190 k, Wrangler 175 k, HD 170 k and minivans 140 k are operating cases with explicit alternatives, not model-fitted estimates.
- Older favourable Asbury commentary is 2025 evidence; the retrieved 2026 release contains no Stellantis-specific confirmation. Profitable fresh replenishment remains unverified [R_OPERATOR, N15].

### Ram light-duty pickups including Classic

**History.** 2018/19 combined pickups 536,980/633,694; no reliable US LD/Classic/DT allocation. Two-generation breadth and product cycle make peak total an unsuitable LD target. Pandemic supply stress is background; pre 2024 total pickup decline to 468,344 in 2022 and 444,926 in 2023 predates the Hurricane switch. No family-specific pandemic lost-sales allocation established. 2024 LD 187,013; refreshed 1500 launch and upper-trim availability failed to ramp cleanly; Classic wind-down also removed a separate value proposition. Neither explains every lost sale. 2025 LD 204,139 (+9.2%); early retail recovery predates returning HEMI deliveries, so HEMI is an additional repair rather than sole cause. 2026 Q1/Q2/Q3 59,828/65,669/76,650; Q3 total +72.8% versus retail +42%. Strong recent pace includes channel effects.

**Customer and dealer mechanism.** Customers choose among full-size F-150/Silverado/Sierra/Tundra, used pickups, smaller trucks or delaying replacement. Ram offers cabin/ride/trim choice and restored V8 identity; buyers need not prefer the highest-output engine. Express and Tradesman address entry payments, while missing premium trims previously obstructed different buyers. Warranty limits uncovered repair exposure for eligible first owners but leaves lost time uncovered and does not directly follow the truck into resale. Anecdotes show both Hurricane acceptance and eTorque concerns; no engine-level demand share or failure rate is inferred. Fresh factory-order solicitation with deposits and initially no OEM rebates is evidence at least one dealer seeks future units, beyond clearing old stock. A factory-sold configuration reduces speculative stock/markdown exposure, but no completion or margin is observed. March order-lockable payment support helps convert payment-sensitive buyers and protects them from lead-time offer risk. July clearance support demonstrates stock selling still requires meaningful concessions at that dealer. Dealer-funded discount and OEM rebate are distinct costs. Faster retail turn can reduce holding expense; nationwide profitable voluntary replenishment remains unproved.

**Repair status.** HEMI: implemented; H2 2025 customer sales reported in prior official audit; Express: listed current MY26; exact dealer trim penetration unknown; MY 2027 orders: dealer solicitation June 18 and intermediary OPEN July 7; not nationwide deliveries; Rumble Bee: orders/initial allocation reported sold out; Q4 2026 dealer arrival planned; included in LD; TRX REEV next generation: retain within family; no separately verified mature delivery pace

**Numerical construction.** Q2+Q3 actual sales of 142,319 imply 284,638 over twelve months; round to 285 k. This uses both quarters rather than carrying peak Q3 alone. YTD annualized 269.5 k and Q3 annualized 306.6 k bracket the 2027 level. It assumes broadly normal annual seasonality, not a statistical seasonal adjustment. 2028 maintenance at 285 k requires defending the recovered position against new GM pickups; there is no second HEMI uplift.

**Alternative outcomes.** 2027 low 270 k retains the YTD pace; high 307 k sustains Q3. 2028 low 260 k approximates the trailing 263 k level; high 320 k exceeds Q3 pace and requires further share gains. Endpoints are operating cases, not independently estimated probabilities.

**Competing explanations.** Q3 total growth exceeds retail growth; fleet mix or scope can account for part of the increment. Brand inventory remained elevated in June; not an LD-specific measurement. Returning HEMI and discounting coexist; neither identifies incremental engine demand. Fresh-order dealer is a self-described volume specialist, not representative of all franchises.

**Price and mix.** Base assumes ongoing competitive support and no automatic same-configuration net-price improvement. Affordable entry/fleet mix can dilute average realization while performance derivatives enrich it; net balance unknown. The July $14 k–$21 k figures combine OEM and dealer money and cannot be subtracted wholesale from manufacturer RPU. No manufacturer actual RPU, invoice margin or matched transaction-price trend is established.

**Would change the view.** Following HEMI anniversary, comparable retail pace and fresh order completions fall despite available configurations, or stable sales require rising manufacturer support/aged stock. Conversely several quarters of low inventory and matched retail/share gains would justify upward revision.

Evidence: N07, N08, N11, R_RAM. Conditional judgment anchored to observed volumes; no fitted elasticity or independently validated 2028 point estimate.

### Ram heavy-duty pickups; 2500/3500, chassis scope unresolved

**History.** Pre 2024 national HD split unavailable; Canada and Washington proxies cannot allocate US losses. 2024 HD 186,107 versus 2025 169,920(-8.7%). Q1 supplier transition confirms a supply problem, but all four 2025 quarters were below 2024; not just one quarter. Q1 2025 HD retail+18% while total-12.7% shows channel/coverage effects. 2026 Q1 38,597, Q2 43,497, Q3 41,107; YTD+3.4% but Q3-6.0%.

**Customer and dealer mechanism.** Commercial towing/upfit buyers and recreational towing households face different use cases. Diesel torque, smoother gear spacing, cold starts and easier service matter to load-bearing work; a lighter-duty or gasoline truck can have lower acquisition/maintenance expense if capacity is unnecessary. Cummins confirms top-access filters and software servicing changes, but actual shop hours saved and fuel-cost advantage are unmeasured. Ford Super Duty and GM HD compete for the same jobs; brand/service relationships and correct axle/body/payload matter more than peak horsepower alone. Pickup and chassis-cab ratings differ. Dealer reordering depends on exact cab/axle/diesel/upfit orders and fleet scheduling. More predictable order windows can reduce unwanted inventory; a contract-backed unit differs from a speculative premium diesel. July schedule lists orders open and 12–14 weeks; not filled orders. August 21 k premium-HD ad warns that high MSRP does not guarantee dealer gross. Invoice, holdback, dealer cash, fleet concessions and completed sale margins are unavailable. Serviceability can strengthen repeat commercial relationships, without proving lower warranty claims.

**Repair status.** 2025 refresh: implemented and selling; supplier specifications confirmed; 2027 orders: open in July 7 intermediary table; July 20 SOP scheduled, execution not separately audited; new generation: planned within 2030; no exact 2028 customer launch assumed

**Numerical construction.** 170 k rounds 2025 actual 169,920 and sits between current YTD/Q3 pace 164 k and trailing 174 k. Restoring 186 k would recover the 2024 level; it is an upside scenario, not a presumed outcome of the refresh. Hold 170 k in 2028 because neither the supplier improvement nor current commercial data quantifies further gains.

**Alternative outcomes.** Low 164 k in 2027 is current nine-month pace; low 160 k in 2028 allows further softening. High 186 k is an observed prior annual outcome. GM HD weakness reduces confidence that all Ram weakness is a unique launch problem.

**Competing explanations.** Q3 decline persists long after initial 2025 changeover. Supply schedule still has lead time and may be stale; not proof every configuration is freely available. Large selective discounts do not establish all HD demand weakness. Warranty is not downtime insurance and first-owner retail terms cannot be applied indiscriminately to fleets.

**Price and mix.** Base requires competitive business purchase/financing support, with no automatic diesel or premium mix uplift. Diesel capability can justify a price premium only for relevant jobs. Manufacturer net realization and actual dealer margin unknown. Treat 2500/3500 plus potential chassis-cab coverage consistently with reported HDPU; no extra 4500/5500 volume added without scope confirmation.

**Would change the view.** Post-refresh family retail and commercial contract deliveries fail while peer HD recovers; or matched stock/discount data show faster turn and sustained growth, supporting upside.

Evidence: N07, N09, R_RAM. Conditional judgment anchored to observed volumes; no fitted elasticity or independently validated 2028 point estimate.

### Grand Cherokee including L and 4xe

**History.** 2018–19: 224,908 to 242,969 sales; established two-row franchise, before L broadened family coverage. Peak is not a fixed demand entitlement. 2021–23: L/new generation and 4xe broaden offer during supply disruption. 2023 family 244,594; powertrain promotion and geographic allocation shaped mix. 2024: 216,148 total; 4xe explains 18,094 of 28,446 lost units arithmetically. Non-4xe recovered in H2 while 4xe fell sharply. Retail/fleet divergence prevents calling all loss household abandonment. 2025: 210,082. 2026 Q1/Q2/Q3: 53,482/48,255/38,027. H1+2.1% Yo Y versus Q3-30.3% supports a new quarterly disruption; magnitude attribution remains open. Current: 4xe production ended. 2026 refresh preserves V6 entry choice and adds turbo four above it; later SRT/refresh dates not firm.

**Customer and dealer mechanism.** For buyers who need two or three rows, towing, winter traction and a more premium cabin, GC/L competes with Explorer, Pilot/Telluride-class family SUVs and used premium SUVs. L expands utility but cannot be treated as entirely new demand. The turbo four adds torque and equipment on regular gasoline, while retained V6 reduces forced-engine-switch risk. A shopper without towing/off-road needs can obtain family space or hybrid economy elsewhere, including Cherokee. Used GC prices and trade-in equity compete with new payments. Former 4xe subsidized lessees may convert to ICE, competitors or delay; conversion is not 100%. A dealer can replenish broadly useful Laredo/Limited and L configurations if customer payment and fresh-stock turn cover acquisition, support and carrying cost. Old 4xe clearance is a separate decision: ended production and recall/repair status affect saleability and residual exposure. Simplifying trims can lower wrong-configuration inventory but cannot cure a too-high payment. Government-bid support demonstrates channel-specific economics, not retail margin. Summer stocking and elevated brand inventory mean a factory restart alone cannot justify unrestricted receipts.

**Repair status.** Implemented/current: refreshed equipment and turbo four offer, V6 entry retained; 4xe no longer produced.; Management explanation: planned downtime; no actual daily plant-output series obtained.; Planned: additional refresh/SRT during 2030 plan, no separately quantified 2028 uplift.

**Numerical construction.** 2026 YTD adjusted only by the 2025 Q4 share gives 190,388; trailing sales 195,625 and unadjusted YTD 186,352 surround 190 k. H1 pace 203,474 is an upside check, not the base. 2025 seasonality itself includes channel effects and the choice to reuse it is an assumption. Maintain 190 k in 2028 before the separately displayed launch-substitution sensitivity; do not assume a second shutdown rebound or recovery to 244.6 k.

**Alternative outcomes.** Low 152 k approximates Q3 x4 and represents persistent sell-through weakness; 2028 low 150 k rounds it. High 210 k/216 k recalls 2025/2024 annual levels and requires evidence of post-downtime retail replenishment without materially higher support.

**Competing explanations.** H1 2026 already about 203,474 annualized; this supports a viable franchise despite Q3 weakness. Q3 NA shipment loss is not the same geography or population as US sales loss 16,526. Some prior 4xe buyers were incentive-driven; a cheaper gasoline offer may retain them, so ended 4xe production is not a one-for-one family loss.

**Price and mix.** Base volume requires competitive financing and affordable configuration availability; lower 4xe share and Laredo mix can lower family average invoice before incentives. Rich L/Summit or SRT mix is an upside, not an automatic offset. No verified OEM realized dollars/unit; no positive price assumption solely from refresh.

**Would change the view.** Two quarters above ≈17 k monthly at stable effective payments and acceptable fresh-stock turns support upside; continued ≈13 k monthly despite available Laredo/Limited supply rejects base. Need sales by powertrain/channel and delivered configuration, not another factory announcement.

Evidence: N09, N12, R_GC, R_SHIP. Conditional judgment anchored to observed volumes; no fitted elasticity or independently validated 2028 point estimate.

### Wrangler including former 4xe

**History.** 2018–19: 240,032/228,032; JL transition-era scale precedes Bronco re-entry and later high-price mix. 2022–24: 181,409/156,581/151,163; 4xe and trims changed the proposition while direct off-road competition broadened. No causal lost-unit split established. 2025: 167,322, already+10.7%; 2026 YTD 131,011, +2.3%, Q3 44,757. Recovery is not a future initiative to add again. Current: 4xe production ended; 2026 monthly editions/performance variants defend interest within the same family.

**Customer and dealer mechanism.** Open-air body, trail capability, personalization and enthusiast identity distinguish Wrangler from ordinary crossovers. Bronco is the direct new-vehicle alternative; used Wranglers can supply similar recreation at lower upfront cost, while practical SUVs win on daily comfort and payments. The discontinued 4xe offer removes the subsidized electric-commute path; gasoline familiarity and engine choice can retain enthusiasts but do not replicate every former lease payment. Edition demand may be substitution from standard trims. Dealers benefit from a recognizable enthusiast franchise and targeted configurations, but expensive customized stock ties up capital when buyers negotiate against used vehicles and Bronco. Reordering requires actual contribution after finance/cash support, not headline MSRP premium. 4xe residual stock and older recalled vehicles must be tracked separately from new gasoline replenishment. Accessory/service opportunities are plausible but not quantified.

**Repair status.** Current: gasoline lineup and limited editions; 4xe production ended.; Recall: battery issue specific to PHEV; separate steering connection campaign covers older Wrangler/Gladiator.; Heritage redesign/Scrambler and SRT plans lack a verified incremental 2028 delivery calendar.

**Numerical construction.** 2026 YTD annualized 174,681 supports 175 k; H1 pace 172,508 and Q3 pace 179,028 are nearby. 2025 actual 167,322 provides the lower recent annual comparison.175 k in 2028 means maintaining this franchise despite Bronco competition, not assuming share recapture or a halo-edition multiplier.

**Alternative outcomes.** 2027 limits 167 k–179 k come from prior annual/current Q3 anchors. 2028 155 k approximates 2023/24 weak levels; 190 k requires higher sustainable demand than any 2026 pace observed here. Wider horizon warrants wider uncertainty, not an invented mean-growth adjustment.

**Competing explanations.** 2026 YTD growth argues against broad nameplate rejection. Exact production-end timing and old-stock sell-through overlap are unresolved, so it does not establish growth after the exit. June 2026 steering recall concerns vehicles built through Dec 2024; it does not establish a 2026 assembly shortage. No matched Bronco versus Wrangler current payments or national order-profit panel recovered.

**Price and mix.** Competitive cash/lease support remains necessary. Lower 4xe mix can reduce sticker average while also changing manufacturer support; net margin direction cannot be inferred. Limited editions can aid mix only at real turn, not stock accumulation.

**Would change the view.** Base fails if normal available gasoline stock cannot sustain ≈15 k/month without increasing support/age; upside requires durable conquest rather than edition mix or fleet batching.

Evidence: N09, N13, R_JEEP. Conditional judgment anchored to observed volumes; no fitted elasticity or independently validated 2028 point estimate.

A functioning paid-off van competes directly with a new-car payment. Owner discussion identifies repair concerns and purchase deferral, not an estimated failure rate. Sienna offers hybrid fuel economy without charging; Pacifica PHEV exit narrows the choice for buyers prioritizing operating costs. A known minivan with differentiated utility can justify stocking ordinary family configurations; separable packages may reduce payment objections. However, brand-level high inventory and unknown retail/fleet split prevent calling the latest increase profitable replenishment. A fresh MY 2027 offer can require markdowns on MY 2026 stock. The MY 2027 refresh was scheduled for summer 2026 and is already partly in the observation window; it is not another full new-product event in 2027. Cheaper configurations and PHEV exit may lower family mix; reduced bundling can improve affordability without a nominal base-price cut. Competitive incentives remain in base case; no automatic discount withdrawal or positive net-price uplift.

### Ram ProMaster large van

**History.** 2018/19 sales 46,600/56,409. 2023 81,663, then 2024 65,869 and 2025 57,591. Public work has not identified whether the 2023 peak reflected fleet batch timing, temporary availability or lasting demand; do not assign causality. 2026 YTD 44,030(-2.8%); Q3 16,314(+4.4%) shows stabilization, not a proven new growth cycle.

**Customer and dealer mechanism.** Operators optimize delivered/upfitted price, usable load space, entry effort, route accessibility, downtime and resale. Low loadfloor and wide cargo area can reduce physical work per delivery; large van capacity avoids repeated trips. Ford Transit, Mercedes Sprinter, used vans and smaller vans are relevant by route/upfit, not merely brand. City will fit some lower-volume urban routes better; big ProMaster retains a capacity use case. EV economics depend on depot charging and route duty cycle; no verified fleet saving supports a separate EV volume premium. Commercial dealer should order against route/upfit needs and credible fleet replacement schedules. Factory supply, bodybuilder completion and customer acceptance all affect cash-cycle length. Merchants lists ICE variants and BEV open with 12–14 week planning leadtimes, supporting orderability but not demand. Aftermarket body/parts availability and workshop uptime support repeat business; none of these imply measured dealer unit margin. Avoid speculative volume merely to fill commercial stock.

**Repair status.** 2027 model: orderable per July schedule; not a wholly new generation; City: companion launch Q1 2027 planned; next generation: no verified 2028 availability; globallargevan 2029 SOP does not establish NA timing

**Numerical construction.** YTD annualized 58,707, TTM56,325 and 2019 56,409 support 58 k; Q3 x4 65,256 is the stronger case. 2023 81,663 did not persist and has no verified backlog mechanism for restoration. City-related substitution is in the separate portfolio sensitivity, not another hidden negative factor.

**Alternative outcomes.** 55 k approximates H1 pace 55,432; 65 k approximates Q3 pace; 2028 high 66 k approximates 2024 actual 65,869. Low 50 k requires weaker fleet renewals or substantial City substitution.

**Competing explanations.** 2023 high has not persisted; 2026 YTD still lower. City can replace some smaller large-van purchases. No transparent contract backlog, retail/fleet split or matched incentive data. Vehicle warranty repair coverage is not compensation for interrupted routes.

**Price and mix.** Base volume assumes ongoing fleet competitiveness, broadly stable comparable-configuration net realization rather than a pricing recovery. Mix across body sizes, fleet agreements and EV/ICE could dominate family RPU, currently unknown. Upfit revenue need not accrue to Stellantis; do not count third-party body cost as OEM vehicle revenue.

**Would change the view.** Fleet renewals and upfit completions fall or City cannibalizes more large-van demand; conversely repeat orders with matched turn and net pricing would support growth.

Evidence: R_RAM, N09. Conditional judgment anchored to observed volumes; no fitted elasticity or independently validated 2028 point estimate.

### Gladiator

**History.** 2019 launch-year 40,047 is partial-year and not mature capacity. 2023: 55,188; 2024: 42,123 after product/price/competitive pressure; no evidence assigns the decline to one cause. 2025: 56,790 with unusually strong Q4 18,277; 2026 Q1/Q2/Q3: 10,087/8946/11,911, YTD-19.7%. 2025 rebound did not establish a durable annual level.

**Customer and dealer mechanism.** Open-air Jeep utility and pickup bed distinguish Gladiator for recreation; Wrangler substitutes when a bed is unnecessary. Tacoma, Ranger and Colorado are alternative utility purchases; discounted Ram 1500 can also compete at similar payment. Buyers prioritizing quiet highway use, fuel economy or work hauling may not pay a Jeep-lifestyle premium. Current advertised 3.6 V6/automatic and 19 mpg combined put fuel/payment costs into the choice; absence of a new powertrain is not evidence of rejection of an unavailable alternative. Expensive off-road trims need customer-specific stocking. Dealers can make an old-stock bargain attractive without that proving they can reorder fresh vehicles at the same net cost. The Idaho bid illustrates acquisition/support arithmetic: invoice 38,479+destination 1995-government support 3000+1000 margin=38,474; that 1000 is quoted gross markup before holding/selling costs, for government buyers only. A broad retail forecast cannot borrow it.

**Repair status.** Current: automatic/V6 lineup and limited editions.; Refresh/redesign within 2030 plan, undated; no independent 2027–28 lift assigned.; Possible Dakota arrival in 2028 increases internal midsize-pickup overlap; no percentage cannibalization invented.

**Numerical construction.** 2025-seasonality-adjusted YTD45,629 and Q3 pace 47,644 support 45 k, balanced by H1 pace 38,066. 2024 actual 42,123 is corroborating scale, not evidence the same causes recur. 2028 central 45 k is before explicit Dakota-substitution sensitivity; it is not an assertion that new competition has no effect.

**Alternative outcomes.** 38 k is H1 pace; 57 k approximates 2025 actual 56,790. 2028 low 35 k allows Dakota/category pressure. Price cuts in 2024 did not prevent the decline, so discounts alone do not establish the upside.

**Competing explanations.** Q3 sequential increase shows some demand; no reason to project permanent collapse. One government quote and unverified dealer website promotions cannot establish national replenishment margin. Recall on older production does not establish current new-unit stop-sale.

**Price and mix.** Base needs continued affordability support; a volume rebound with richer trims and fewer discounts simultaneously is unproven. Lower-priced variants or sustained support could lift units but must be charged once to net realization/cost.

**Would change the view.** Fresh model-year Gladiators repeatedly selling at ≈4 k/month with unchanged support would justify higher base; strong retail discounts plus continued age accumulation reject profitable recovery.

Evidence: N14, R_JEEP. Conditional judgment anchored to observed volumes; no fitted elasticity or independently validated 2028 point estimate.

### Compass

**History.** 2018–19: 171,167/143,934; pre-pandemic erosion already existed. 2022–24: 86,175/96,173/111,697: growth during a period of wider Jeep contraction, so not every unit is a recoverable shortage. 2025: 101,997. 2026 Q1-41.5%, Q2-20.1%, Q3-61.5%; H1 lost 17,808 before Q3 planned downtime. 2026 YTD 47,837, Q3 10,811. Returning Cherokee adds a larger hybrid choice above Compass and potential internal substitution.

**Customer and dealer mechanism.** Compact size, AWD/off-road image and a lower ticket than Cherokee/GC provide a role. ## Forecast method and limits

A small retrospective check illustrates the danger of the anchor method itself: Q3 2024 annualized would have underpredicted 2025 Ram LD sales by 21.1% and overpredicted Ram HD by 15.1%. Only two cases are available here; this is not an error distribution or validation of the new estimates. The audit cannot turn recent pace into certainty merely by removing percentage overlays.

Recent pace, trailing sales and a reused seasonal distribution are alternative transformations of overlapping observations. They are not three independent proofs. Annualizing the latest quarter is a scale check, especially weak around fleet batches or launch interruptions. 2025-pattern calculations are explicitly labeled and should not be used as reliable seasonal adjustments where product transitions dominate.

Existing-product central forecasts assume a broadly stable market environment and continued competitive financing. This is a null planning condition, not a sourced macro forecast. No generic+1% industry tailwind or fitted interest-rate sensitivity is embedded. Product-specific downside/upside cases provide operating alternatives but are not a quantified recession/boom model.

The launch comparison is deliberately two-sided. Envista demonstrates meaningful uptake of an affordable new crossover; Hornet demonstrates that an announced product and dealer distribution can still fail. Trax is a stronger outcome but had an established Chevrolet franchise and a different starting price, so its 200.7 k 2024 sales are not assigned to new Chrysler vehicles. Selected GMsmall SUV sales grew in aggregate even while some siblings fell; this does not identify individual buyer substitution.

The compact Ram 10 k, Recon scaling upside, GLH/Ram SUV sensitivities and Chrysler product allocation are the weakest numerical components. They remain explicit scenarios. A precise dealer-arrival month, price/trim availability and real retail orders would improve them more than additional general product commentary.

## Quarterly and financial use

quarterly-scenarios.json repeats observed 2025 quarter distributions only for selected continuing families and separately identifies assumed launch ramps. Other quarter cells remain unpopulated. Quarterly sums reconcile to annual amounts, but that arithmetic is not validation of within-year timing. 2026 Q4 and prior 2026 full-year assumptions are preserved in the baseline, not silently presented as actuals.

Customer sales are not wholesale shipments. Genuine replenishment would make shipments track customer demand more closely; destocking or launch stocking can cause divergence. No model-level dealer-stock series or unmatched shipment-minus-sales residual is invented. Manufacturer net RPU remains an estimated financial input, not MSRP or dealer discount. More entry vehicles can improve units while diluting average selling price. No extra mix uplift or discount removal is automatically awarded.
