## Findings that sharpen or correct our earlier interpretation

### Pricing is carryover, not repeated future price increases

**Management statement:** CFO explicitly says no additional price increases are assumed in the plan. The positive price contribution versus 2025 comes from actions already taken: positive in NA, MEA and South America; negative in Europe. T pp31–32; public Q&A pp6–8.

The CFO says roughly half of the approximately two-point group top-line AOI-margin contribution is pricing, principally NA carryover. This is not a two-percent annual price increase, nor a model-specific RPU target. Management assumes neutral NA profit mix versus 2025 over the later plan years: favorable regulatory effects offset lower-segment vehicles. Group mix is also neutral as NA geographic growth offsets European LEV dilution. A neutral profit-mix assumption does not mathematically imply unchanged revenue per vehicle.

**Correction:** our earlier description of management expecting general pricing/mix help was insufficiently precise. Do not populate annual price escalators as though guided. Test whether the existing net-price level can be retained as competitors respond and incentives change.

### The growth plan needs market-share gains and late launches

Management assumes a flat industry through 2030, with American-brand volumes rising about 35%, market coverage 60% to90%, 11 new nameplates and12 refreshed models. Coverage is presence in addressable segments, not sales share or demonstrated consumer conversion. T pp11–15; D pp57,61–62,81.

Management's claim that relevant-segment share is15.6%, versus7.6% across the US market, does not establish that new entries can immediately earn15.6% share. Their incumbent strengths and competitive intensity can differ.

The explicit slide timing for compact and midsize pickups is **start of production2028** (D p42/technology slide14). T pp31/35 describes late2027–2029 activity and2028 acceleration, including Chrysler launches and pickup ramps. Exact quarterly start dates and individual Chrysler dates are not disclosed. Do not turn a2030 lineup into full-year2028 sales.

### VCP has a quality category missing from the three-driver shorthand

F p18/finance slide9 explicitly shows **components; manufacturing; quality; logistics/indirect costs**. The later three-driver explanation used in our architecture is not a complete disclosure crosswalk. T pp24–25,29–30,33.

Important management claims:

- EUR6bn annual/run-rate savings versus a2025 baseline by2028, roughly balanced between NA and Europe; NA section says more thanEUR3bn.
- Components are75% of **new-vehicle cost**, not75% of revenue or necessarily75% of consolidated cost of sales.
- Component reviews target more than80% of the NA/Europe component cost base in2026; first wave covers about35%. These are review coverage, not savings percentages or realized completion.
- CFO uses total cost of sales aboveEUR100bn as a scale comparison. This does not disclose three/four separate regional addressable cost pools.
- Forty percent of initiatives intended to be implemented late2026, withEUR2.4bn benefiting2027. Initiatives, savings values and quarterly realization are not identical measures.
- Management expects fullEUR6bn AOI/cash benefit; raw-material headwinds are separately modeled. Whether competition forces giveback remains an unanswered investment question.
- Existing cost structure and product benchmarking underpin VCP; partnerships may improve execution, but the announced savings are not simply contingent future partnership synergies.

**Model implication, not an architecture edit:** retain the user's equal-quarter allocation and annual reduction assumptions. Before population, explicitly map quality/warranty and indirect costs into the agreed categories or an identified reconciliation. Do not silently omit them, double count them, or add a fourth modeled driver without flagging the change. Lower current warranty claims do not immediately equal the same percentage reduction in warranty expense/cash.

## Full presentation notes

### Governance and capital allocation — T pp4–8

Regional P&L accountability and locally embedded brands replace a more centralized operating approach; leadership team shrinks30 to15. Management says quality and industrial execution improved, but those are outcome claims requiring comparable definitions and cohorts. Four global brands lead shared-asset rollouts: Jeep, Ram, Peugeot, Fiat. Five regional brands use those assets with differentiation. Specialty-brand treatment and Maserati strategy are separate.

More thanEUR60bn investment over2026–30:40% global assets/technology,60% brands/products. Around60% of product investment goes to NA, approximatelyEUR22bn—not60% of all investment. About30% of product investment expands market coverage;70% concentrates on global brands. Capital allocation creates capacity to launch, not proof customers will buy.

### Platforms and technology — T pp8–11

STLA One starts2027, addresses B/C/D segments over time, consolidates five architectures and targets around20% cost reduction on applicable vehicles. It is not a20% cut to all group costs. Three global platforms target50% of2030 volumes; Q&A clarifies halving overall platform count is around2035, not automatically2030. Development target falls from roughly44 months to24 months. Shared platforms may lower entry-model cost but introduce common launch/quality risks.

REEV full-size SUV/pickup technology has2026 SOP on D p42. That is a platform-family statement, not verified SOP for every named derivative. STLA Brain launches in Europe2027 and US2028 in remarks; SmartCockpit dates/names are partly garbled. Driver-assistance rollouts2027–28 and cost reduction are product features and potential economics, not separately disclosed volume targets. Software revenue is not quantified in euros; avoid a fabricated major profit contribution.

### North America product economics — T pp11–15

Chrysler's three crossovers address practical/affordable buyers; management claims approximately35% of shoppers prioritize these attributes. This is positioning evidence, not a measured accessible customer pool uniquely available to Chrysler. Arrow/Arrow Cross and Airflow appear on D p79; precise mapping of unnamed spoken platform descriptions to names is inferred, not directly named in the transcript.

Jeep combines Cherokee's return, Recon, refreshed mainstream SUVs and an expanded Wrangler family. Ram combines existing-truck powertrain/variant restoration, smaller pickups, vans and an SUV. SRT adds premium variants across existing nameplates and targets3k to50k units, with2–3x donor-model margin claimed. SRT units are inside brand totals; do not add50k on top. Margin multiple lacks enough definition for a euro-per-unit coefficient.

Affordability slide D p80 specifies9 models with starting MSRP below$39k by2030 versus2 in2025, including2 below$29k. Spoken$40k/$30k is rounded. Starting MSRP is neither transaction price nor manufacturer net revenue. Entry price matters only if attractive configurations are actually available at meaningful volume.

Tim acknowledges Ram inventory grew to rebalance mix and prepare for growth (T p13). That is management's rationale for stocking, not evidence of subsequent sell-through. CFO/CEO later identify DS/Classic removal as an affordability gap and describe Black Express as a response (T p28). Do not use the loose 'three years ago' timing as a verified discontinuation date.

### Europe — T pp15–19

Differentiate brands, expand market coverage, reduce BEV costs and raise utilization from60% to80%. Planned capacity reduction is800k without plant shutdowns, through repurposing, sharing and volume. STLA One and the subEUR15k e-car from2028 support affordability; partnerships offer sourcing/industrial capacity benefits. BEV penetration dilutes mix in the CFO's forecast. Region target is15% revenue growth and3–5% AOI margin by2030; transcript references to50%/35% are errors or concatenation, not guidance. Regulatory easing is hoped for, not enacted by the speech. Claims of resilience without easing and reliance on regulation deserve separate scenarios.

### South America — T p20

Protect entry-level Fiat, expand pickups, renew Jeep, localize Leapmotor and use Asian sourcing in import-heavy Andean markets. Management targets10% revenue growth and8–10% margin by2030. Local incumbency is the proposed defense against Chinese entrants, not evidence that competition will be harmless. Regional Dakota/Rampage announcements must not be treated as proof of US launch dates.

### Middle East and Africa — T pp20–21

Move competitively sourced volumes from about30% to90% through local production and Asian imports; target75% execution by2028. Approximately22 car lines target90% of sales; partners/local incentives support capital efficiency. Target40% revenue growth and10–12% margin by2030. Group treatment depends on consolidated versus JV activity; avoid adding JV sales as fully consolidated revenue.

### Asia Pacific and partnerships — T pp21–22

Leapmotor International, Dongfeng/DPCA and Tata are the capital-light sourcing strategy. Management cites180k Leapmotor International vehicles in2027 and100k globally sold localized partner vehicles in2028. APAC targets doubled revenue and4–6% margin by2030. Export sales may be recognized where sold; cumulative partner-related sales over several years are not annual APAC revenue. No US launch of these Asian-sourced Jeeps is established by these general export statements.

### Financial services — T pp22–23

Management cites overEUR85bn managed average receivables in2025 and overEUR21bn US portfolio, with762 average retail FICO. Captive financing is presented as an affordability, loyalty and replacement-cycle tool. Claimed up-to20% loyalty advantage and three-year earlier return compare selected finance cohorts; not causal proof that financing itself creates those differences.

Funding-cost improvements, penetration, insurance/ancillary sales and expansion underpin aboveEUR1.5bn2030 AOI. CFO introductory wording says incremental, but SFS CEO explicitly describes total contribution; preserve ambiguity and do not addEUR1.5bn to an existing base blindly.2025 weakness includes UK redress and residual-value impairments on two unnamed discontinued US nameplates. Parent net dividends start2028 and targetEUR500m2030. Credit losses and residuals remain economically relevant despite the 'stabilizer' framing.

2028 group targets:EUR175bn revenue,5% AOI margin,EUR3bn industrial FCF;2030:EUR190bn,7%,EUR6bn. NA2030 targets25% revenue growth and8–10% AOI margin; no distinct NA2028 margin target established here. Approximately60% of planned group profit increase comes from NA.

## Corrections and boundaries

- Correct transcription 'Bumblebee' to **Rumble Bee** using the public company transcript. Model year2027 does not independently establish calendar SOP.
- **Ramcharger on the2030 showroom map is a large SUV**, distinct from the unnamed range-extended pickup discussion. Do not merge them because of historical name usage.
- D p79 has a further unlabeled covered Dodge vehicle with addition/SRT symbols. Retain an unidentified row; do not invent its name or independently add volumes.
- Do not force every icon, body style and powertrain into a one-to-one23-model/11-new count. Brand targets are disclosed; per-nameplate targets are not.
- The3MIS quality metric is early service claims, not lifetime failure rate, and differs from warranty cash cost. Production effectiveness differs from utilization on three-shift installed capacity.
- Reread found materially sharper pricing and VCP qualifications. Earlier review files remain historical records; these notes supersede their less precise statements on those points.
