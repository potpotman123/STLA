# Transformation cost: correcting the payroll-only anchor

14 October 2026. User challenges the incomplete manufacturing denominator and requests component-allocation diligence. Payroll was a measurable subset, not an appropriate estimate of all transformation costs. No savings forecasts changed.

## Full perimeter

| Subpool | Cost content | Mechanism relevant to savings |
|---|---|---|
| Plant labor | Production staff, supervisors, plant engineering, external labor and overtime | Fewer hours per good unit; less overtime/contract labor; actual staffing changes |
| Maintenance and repairs | Spare parts, external servicing, repairs and routine preventive maintenance | Fewer breakdowns, better maintenance planning, lower emergency repair expense; cutting preventive maintenance is not automatically sustainable |
| Energy/utilities | Electricity, gas, water, compressed air, heating and cooling | Lower consumption per good unit, reduced idle running, energy procurement |
| Other plant overhead/consumables | Cleaning, security, tooling consumables, noncapital repairs and site overhead | Lower external purchasing and resource use; keep each item in one VCP workstream |
| Depreciation and relevant amortization | Period expense for equipment, buildings, capitalized tooling and eligible factory systems | Changes in asset footprint and future depreciation; not an automatic proportional response to better operating efficiency |

Purchased vehicle parts remain components. External freight/warehousing remains logistics unless an explicit mapping says otherwise. Maintenance labor already included in plant payroll must not be added a second time with external maintenance expense. Internal rework/scrap must be assigned once between manufacturing and quality.

## New observed anchors

2025 annual report, PDF p258, Note 11 property, plant and equipment: depreciation 3.361bn plant/machinery/equipment; 0.594bn buildings. Sum 3.955bn. These are group asset classes, not a certified manufacturing-only subset or an NA allocation. Land depreciation 0.003bn relates to its accounting perimeter (including rights of use); other-assets depreciation 1.855bn includes nonfactory assets, so do not automatically add it. Right-of-use depreciation disclosed separately is already within the total PP&E movement; no duplicate addition.

PDF p213: machinery useful lives 2–25 years; buildings 33–40. Subsequent expenditure is capitalized only when it increases future economic benefits, otherwise expensed. Distinguish annual depreciation from capital investment and asset carrying value. Maintenance cash expense and capitalized upgrades therefore need different treatment.

2025 expanded sustainability statement p49: total energy consumption 12,577,752 MWh (12.578 TWh). It includes several fuels, electricity and purchased heat/cooling; 2025 scope includes fuel for company-owned logistics trucks. This is physical consumption, not a manufacturing energy bill. No single electricity tariff should be applied to the total. Need geography/fuel-specific volumes and realized prices before estimating annual energy cost.

## Interpretation of the 89% efficiency metric

User-supplied May 2026 Investor Day PDF p9 defines OPE as actual units produced / (gross line capacity in jobs per hour × line running time). The later Q2 call cites about 89%, up 870bp; the Q2 deck p5 corroborates the 870bp improvement. This is not a margin or an 8.7% reduction in the full factory cost base. It is also not calendar-based factory capacity utilization: a line can be effective when running but operate relatively few shifts.

Illustrative arithmetic using the rounded reported levels: prior efficiency approximately 80.3%; 89/80.3 - 1 = 10.8% more output for identical measured line hours, capacity and mix. For identical output, theoretical measured hours fall by 1 - 80.3/89 = 9.8%. Neither is an observed labor-hour or expense reduction. Actual savings depend on paid time, line staffing, overtime, energy, yield and whether hours/costs are removed or redeployed.

Hypothetical machine: 100m purchase price, ten-year straight-line depreciation and no residual value gives 10m annual depreciation. Increasing annual output from 100k to 125k lowers depreciation per unit from 100 to 80 euros but leaves annual depreciation at 10m. This can support profitable growth without a separate 2m absolute cost reduction.

## Model implication

Keep a full accounting transformation base and a smaller/differentiated addressable savings base. Use separate reduction assumptions for labor, maintenance/energy/other cash plant costs, and depreciation only where supported. Do not apply the same VCP haircut to all machinery-related expense. Investment avoidance belongs in capex and future D&A, not immediate AOI. The existing user convention of annual recognized savings divided by four remains unchanged. Higher-volume absorption is separate from same-volume VCP efficiency; reconcile once to the reported Industrial AOI bridge.

## Component allocation: bounded follow-up result

The June 13, 2024 Tech & Operations presentation, visually checked PDF pp19/22, reports 2023 total purchasing/supply-chain purchase value 132bn and direct-material spend 100bn. These are distinct scopes. The regional map on p22 is 41bn of cumulative awards to suppliers in best-cost countries since the merger, NOT annual regional consumption. Its 20bn North American marker cannot establish North America's component allocation. No empirical replacement for the previous 1.5x/2.0x per-vehicle sensitivity was established. Keep these assumed, not calibrated.

PDF pp28–29 also corroborate broad transformation scope: energy, headcount, process efficiency, footprint and asset reuse/capex. Historical management claims include 11% transformation-cost improvement for 2021–23 and 96% overall production effectiveness. These have insufficient matching geography/metric definitions to compare directly to the 2026 NA 89% observation; do not infer deterioration without reconciling scope.
