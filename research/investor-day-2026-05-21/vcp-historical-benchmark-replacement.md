# VCP replacement method: historical outcomes and peer cost levels

14 October 2026. User authorized the simpler architecture and historical comparisons, then requested Nissan/Toyota component-cost comparisons. Architecture updated; workbook and preserved AI baseline unchanged.

## Decision

Use one effective cumulative saving rate per full NA cost pool per forecast year. Timing is already inside that rate; execution expense is separate only when not already netted. Remove the unsupported addressability × reduction construction from the active architecture. Preserve its arithmetic as historical analysis, not the current recommended forecast.

The replacement research has two tests: **historical delivery** (what reductions have actually occurred?) and **cost-level headroom** (what comparable inefficiency remains at STLA?). Neither test currently establishes an independently calibrated components percentage. This is a substantive limitation, not a reason to manufacture a new precise input. No replacement driver rates have been approved or populated.

These are a curated comparison set, not interchangeable statistical observations. Do not assign arbitrary averaging weights. The Nissan annual report corroborates its business-review result; those are not two independent successes. The current Nissan restructuring target is also not evidence of achieved industry-leading costs.

## What do Toyota and Nissan actually spend on components?

| STLA global, calendar 2025 | >€88bn direct-material purchases; 5.484m consolidated shipments. Their ratio is about €16.0k per shipment using €88bn as a floor proxy. | Purchasing/wholesale ratio only: inventory, parts and consolidation scope still matter. Not a measured vehicle BOM. |

Ratio approximately ¥3.793m per unit. | **No.** Numerator is broader than components and includes non-component production costs and other product activity. Do not compare this ratio with STLA's material-only estimate. |
| Nissan FY ended March 2025 | Issuer prospectus defines automotive COGS as materials/parts plus labor, depreciation, logistics, certain R&D and quality. Group COGS is ¥10.940tr and includes financing scope. | **No.** No matched component-only numerator was established. Do not divide total costs by retail sales including unconsolidated ventures. |

Therefore this pass does **not** establish that Toyota or Nissan has component costs X% below Stellantis. “Lower average cost” and “more efficient at producing equivalent content” are different claims. Currency conversion cannot fix a scope mismatch. The search stopped after the primary filings and targeted disclosure checks rather than manufacturing a residual-material estimate.

To identify a usable cost gap, compare equivalent body size, engine/hybrid/battery configuration, drivetrain and equipment. Ram 1500/Tundra, Grand Cherokee/Grand Highlander or Pathfinder, and Compass/RAV4 or Rogue are candidate starting points, not mechanically equivalent vehicles. Differences in in-house engine/parts production versus purchased complete systems must be reconciled. A lower external purchasing bill can coexist with higher internal manufacturing cost. A teardown/BOM study or supplier quote comparison would be more informative than unmatched group COGS; no paid data was purchased.

## Quantitative replacement reference case

The recent Renault observation supports a transparent **reference calculation**, not a component-only forecast:

`€400 annual incremental variable-cost saving per reference vehicle × 1.472m fixed STLA NA reference vehicles`

| Assumed years of equivalent repeated delivery | Annual benefit against starting cost level |
|---|---:|
| One year | €0.589bn |
| Two years | €1.178bn |
| Three years | €1.766bn |

This uses an observed euro-per-vehicle outcome, then explicitly assumes repeat delivery and transferability. Only the initial Renault achievement is historical evidence; repetition and applicability to STLA are assumptions. The arithmetic holds STLA reference volume/mix fixed. The inspected disclosure does not isolate every commodity/content effect within the €400, so this is not a verified constant-input-price productivity rate. Unmatched execution costs, input inflation, FX and selling-price changes need separate reconciliation before forecast admission. It is not an estimate that a euro buys the same saving opportunity in a Ram pickup as in a Renault car.

**Do not assign all €1.766bn to components and then add overlapping variable manufacturing, quality and logistics savings.** The observed perimeter is variable COGS. Nor should it be added on top of the old €2.09bn case. It is a replacement comparison envelope pending a defensible category split. An effective percentage can be calculated only against a matching variable-cost denominator; the assumed €35bn component pool is not that denominator.

This is preferable to inventing a new component percentage, but it is not yet sufficient to populate the individual driver forecasts. Nissan's 20% illustrates a more extensive transformation, not an automatic upside applied to today's STLA purchase base. A simple percentage may ultimately be appropriate; selection still requires matched evidence.

## Driver status and model treatment

- **Components:** old 60%/7% inputs are retired as the preferred estimation method; no empirically calibrated replacement yet. Use the Renault envelope and Nissan transformation as separate reference cases, then require a comparable component denominator or identifiable deployment evidence.
- **Manufacturing:** Renault's plant-specific energy reductions are not reductions in the entire cash-plant pool or payroll. Broad fixed-cost programs do not establish paid-labor savings at constant output. Existing estimates remain unvalidated scenarios, not historical-calibrated rates.
- **Quality:** claims-count improvements and provision movements are not a lifetime expense reduction rate. The inspected Nissan warranty-provision amounts cover different volumes/cohorts; do not interpret their decline as a pure efficiency percentage. No automatic remeasurement release.
- **Logistics/indirect:** a named project supports action, but neither broad cost programs nor old targets establish the current NA rate. Keep unestimated rates distinct from zero.
- **Depreciation:** retain zero operational VCP benefit absent a demonstrated mechanism. This is a deliberate exclusion, not missing-data imputation.

The next discriminating inputs are component-purchase expenses with matched production scope, teardown/engineering cost evidence on comparable models, and achieved savings split by mechanism. The updated architecture can accept those rates without adding another eligibility layer. Current evidence is sufficient for the architecture change and comparative reference case, but insufficient for a credible new full driver-level savings forecast.
