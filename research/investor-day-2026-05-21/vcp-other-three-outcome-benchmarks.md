## 1. Manufacturing: genuine historical result, imperfect transfer

Nissan's issuer Annual Report 2004 (year ended March 2005), printed p51 / PDF p53, reports the 8.3% and 14% outcomes over NISSAN 180. Synchronizing supplier deliveries and production, shortening lead times and reducing waste are plausible parallels to STLA. This is a cumulative program outcome, not an annual rate. The excerpt does not fully reconcile its cost definition with our payroll/cash-plant/depreciation buckets or isolate fixed-cost absorption. The company was growing; do not credit that absorption again in VCP if volume already earns it in the AOI bridge. Printed p12 / PDF p14 reports manufacturing efficiencies offset by Canton expansion costs, increasing manufacturing/logistics expenses by ¥15bn that year. Historical delivery can coexist with higher reported expense.

Source: issuer report hosted by AnnualReports, https://www.annualreports.com/HostedData/AnnualReportArchive/n/OTC_NSANY_2004.pdf . Direct Nissan archive paths failed; primary report content was read on the mirror.

For scale, the 8.3% transfer gives €456.5m on assumed labor and €124.5m on cash plant. These splits are arithmetic, NOT evidence Nissan saved 8.3% in each subcategory. Relative to the full €8.8bn including €1.8bn depreciation, €581m is 6.60%. Depreciation saving stays excluded absent a mechanism. Labor contracts, overtime, staffing and agency costs determine whether fewer hours become lower expense; better OPE alone does not prove a payroll saving.

A more targeted cross-check: Renault reports energy consumption down 26% between 2021 and 2024, and €270m of 2023 savings across AI-enabled logistics/energy efforts. Applying 26% to our earlier assumed €0.3bn energy allowance gives €78m at constant prices, only 5.2% of the €1.5bn cash-plant pool. Energy consumption is not expenditure and this is not necessarily a constant-output measure. It cannot justify cutting all plant costs by 26%. Maintenance, taxes, insurance and services need separate mechanisms. The €270m overlaps categories and lacks a matching denominator, so it is not added.

Source: https://www.renaultgroup.com/en/magazine/technology/artificial-intelligence-and-the-automotive-industry-at-the-heart-of-our-strategy/ (4 September 2025). Renault's 30% ICE /50% EV industrial-cost reduction announcements for 2027 are targets, not achieved benchmarks: https://media.renaultgroup.com/with-re-industry-renault-group-is-launching-an-ambitious-plan-to-transform-its-industrial-base/?lang=fra .

Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/37996/000003799626000015/R134.htm . These are net-of-estimated-supplier-recoveries roll-forward items, not a constant-volume/mix productivity bridge. The reduction in old-warranty revisions cannot be generalized to all warranty expense; cash payments are not current-year AOI cost. Ford's broader material-and-warranty savings claim is compatible with flat aggregate accrual additions, because a cost bridge and an unadjusted total answer different questions.

Implication for STLA: early claim frequency must connect to expected lifetime claim frequency, repair severity, coverage, recoveries and cohorts. Longer Ram warranty coverage can offset technical improvements. The ~€0.9bn retained 2025 NA remeasurement in our €3bn proxy is not automatically reversed. Keep recall/provision normalization distinct from operational VCP and quality expense distinct from in-plant scrap already counted elsewhere. No matched peer outcome in this bounded pass establishes a 10%, 20% or 38% reduction in the whole STLA pool. Prior mature €375m quality scenario equals12.5% of €3bn; it remains an assumption, not historically validated.

## 3. Logistics and indirect: narrow outcomes are stronger than broad promises

4flow reports an automaker consolidated low-volume inbound shipments rather than receiving partly filled LTL loads five times weekly. Transport cost fell17%, utilization rose74%, and a wider rollout was planned. This directly supports routing/load consolidation, but is a vendor-selected anonymous success, not an independent all-network result. IT integration in two months does not prove all savings mature in two months.

Source: https://www.4flow.com/supply-chain-software/integrated-material-and-transportation-planning-software/case-study-ltl .

A 2021 research paper on Renault cross-dock operations reports20% lower inbound transportation cost in the first implemented period without higher other costs. Its indexed abstract distinguishes real implementation from simulation. Full text was not accessed (403); spend mix, observation length and causal controls remain unavailable. It strengthens the mechanism but does not establish STLA transferability.

Source: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1755581721000274 .

A 2022 German-factory optimization study found33.86% modeled cost reduction with570 suppliers and3,927 SKUs. This is an optimization result, not verified realized P&L savings, so excluded from calibration: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1366554522001259 .

Argus reports42% reduction in PREMIUM freight at an anonymous US/Mexico automotive supplier. Emergency expediting is a small special cost pool; cannot apply42% to total freight. Its page title advertises15% logistics cost reduction but the inspected body does not substantiate that aggregate figure, so it is not used. https://arguslogistics.com/case-study-automotive/ .

For reference, €255–300m on logistics alone equals10.2–12.0% of the combined €2.5bn logistics/indirect base IF indirect contributes zero. That zero is a scenario boundary, not a forecast. The old whole-pool mature rate was7.2% (€180m). Current evidence supports logistics opportunity but does not validate either whole-pool rate. Need IT/vendor contract consolidation or actual indirect spend outcomes for the €1bn remainder.
