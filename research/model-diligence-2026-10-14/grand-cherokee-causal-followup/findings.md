# Grand Cherokee: historical cause and recovery audit

14 October 2026 · D095 · US customer sales include retail and fleet. Grand Cherokee is not the smaller discontinued Cherokee. This addendum supersedes any implication that 200,000 is a validated sustainable base; the saved numerical scenario is unchanged.

The best current explanation is a changed customer value proposition and an unstable powertrain/channel mix, with subsequent production adjustments—not an established supply-only interruption that automatically reverses. The causal shares remain unidentified. Ram itself is not a fully identified causal experiment, and more incentives do not demonstrate management confidence.

It was not a continuous post-pandemic collapse. The issuer described 2021 as its best total AND retail Grand Cherokee year since2000 [N1]. That is evidence against assuming the franchise never had healthy household appeal. It does not establish the peak as normal: the new three-row L arrived during2021, the old WK and new WL overlapped, and 4xe arrived later. Compare the offer being sold, not just a nameplate label.

In2023 total sales were0.7% above2019, but included45,6844xe units. The remaining198,910 were18.1% below2019’s total—even though the family now included three-rowL. This is arithmetic evidence that headline stability hid a different composition; not evidence that exactly44,059 former gasoline buyers left. PHEV adoption, internal substitution, fleet mix, supply and body styles all changed.

## 2. A specific customer mechanism: the replacement offer became more expensive and less flexible

The old2022WK LaredoE4WD was advertised at$40,500 includingdestination [N2]. The2023new-generation Laredo4WD was$44,830 [N3]. That$4,330 gap compares different products; the redesigned vehicle added utility/technology. More cleanly, the new-generation gasoline Laredo itself rose$2,710 (6.8%) fromMY2022 toMY2023, with limited other changes; Limited rose$3,210. Trailhawk became4xe-only, starting$67,055. Some customers seeking off-road equipment consequently had to accept the plug-in package and its price or choose something else. This is an intentional offer change, not necessarily an inability to build cars.

Mechanism: a returning owner sees a higher purchase/payment hurdle or an unwanted bundle; more customers compare rival/used SUVs or postpone. This is a plausible source of lost conversion, not an estimated elasticity. Dated prices establish the hurdle; prior owner/buyer anecdotes establish that deal terms and perceived repair risk can matter, not their population prevalence [R3].

Counterevidence: new-generation benefits may justify higher prices to some buyers; GC total sales still rose in2023. In Q42023 Cox reported GC ATP$57,291, down0.4%YoY, while sales rose22% [N5]. Therefore a claim that GC realized prices kept rising throughout2023 is incorrect. Earlier MSRP increases, mix and subsequent discounts must be separated. No constant-quality matched peer payment panel was recovered.

These reuse the verified quarterly issuer decomposition. In H2, non4xe sales recovered9.8% while4xe fell69.4%. For the year,4xe accounted arithmetically for18,094 of28,446 lost units (63.6%). The family did not suffer one uniform rejection of all versions.

The dated commercial sequence is more informative than generic brand sentiment [R1–R3]: gasolineMSRP cuts inJanuary2024 excluded4xe; some rebates disappeared at the same time, so lower net payments are unproven. March-to-April CCAP forum quotations show a PHEV credit component falling from$7,500 to$3,750, while another lender reportedly retained$7,500. That could alter relative4xe economics, but borrower geography, terms and lender penetration are not matched. Regional allocation rules also shaped available powertrains; theMarch regulatory agreement did not itself prove that national4xe shipments fell.

Reliability concerns were present in buyer discussions before theOctober2024 battery alert, but the late alert cannot explain theQ2 onset. Positive owners and incentive-driven purchasers also existed. We cannot allocate the decline to defects, financing or fleet batches quantitatively.

## 4. Production cuts can be a consequence, not the original cause

Cox described excess Stellantis-brand inventory entering2024 and associated production/shift reductions [N6]. It is therefore unsafe to turn every later production decline into evidence that unsatisfied consumer demand was blocked. Conversely, aggregate excess stock can coexist with scarce desired configurations; theSeptember2023 product guide reported limited inventory for popular versions [N4].

The decisive distinction is available sellable stock by configuration. Old, unwanted or recalled stock is not equivalent to the desired vehicle. No consistent national GCtrim/powertrain stock series was obtained. Lotlinx indexed reports offered leads, but full originals returned404; their numerical values are deliberately not admitted here.

## 5. Why 2026 H1 is not yet a sustainable-demand baseline

H1 annualized203,474; Q3 annualized152,108. TheOctober13 release explicitly attributes approximately25k fewer NA GCshipments to planned downtime [L2]. That confirms management’s shipment explanation, not the cause of the16,526 decline in US customer sales. The two counts have different populations.

TheJuly30 call says inventories were built before the shutdown (pp5–6,14), and the CFO says rental sales are more concentrated inH1 (p11) [L1]. These are material counterweights to the old argument: buffers may cushion production stoppages, and H1 can contain more fleet activity. Neither statement provides GC-specific stock or fleet quantities, so neither proves the200k forecast wrong.

The forecast currently requires: restored sellable configurations; an attractive enough effective payment; sufficient retention after4xe exit; and economically viable dealer replenishment. A refresh and factory restart are inputs, not verified outcomes. Keep200k explicitly conditional. Do not mechanically replace it with152k either:Q3 is also potentially distorted. No numerical forecast changed in this audit.
