# Jeep product diligence — 14 October 2026

US customer sales include retail and fleet. This is a proposed scenario revision, not management guidance, a calibrated demand model, or an approved workbook change. The companion JSON preserves sources, dates, scope and calculations.

The principal update is **official confirmation that Grand Cherokee 4xe and Wrangler 4xe are no longer in production**. The older 2026 Grand Cherokee launch release is superseded on that point. The forecast must test gasoline retention and fresh-stock economics; it cannot recover the old plug-in peak merely by assuming supply normalizes. [Jeep current status](https://www.jeep.com/4xe.html)

Compass was already sharply weaker in H1 before Q3 downtime; Grand Cherokee had a healthier H1 before its Q3 break. Cherokee is a useful hybrid segment re-entry, but the affordable-offer and landed-cost problem is unresolved. Wrangler is already recovering. Gladiator has not sustained its 2025 rebound. Wagoneer has regained roughly its 2024 scale without establishing a luxury-mix gain. Wagoneer S and Recon remain explicitly unallocated, with different supply and positioning constraints.

## Model theses

### Grand Cherokee including L and 4xe

**View.** Recover a competitive gasoline family, not the entire historical plug-in peak. A temporary Q3 factory effect is plausible, but premium-payment pressure, ended PHEV production and internal Cherokee substitution cap restoration.

**Historical sequence.** 2018–19: 224908 to 242969 sales; established two-row franchise, before L broadened family coverage. Peak is not a fixed demand entitlement. 2021–23: L/new generation and 4xe broaden offer during supply disruption. 2023 family 244594; powertrain promotion and geographic allocation shaped mix. 2024: 216148 total; 4xe explains 18094 of 28446 lost units arithmetically. Non-4xe recovered in H2 while 4xe fell sharply. Retail/fleet divergence prevents calling all loss household abandonment. 2025: 210082. 2026 Q1/Q2/Q3: 53482/48255/38027. H1+2.1% YoY versus Q3-30.3% supports a new quarterly disruption; magnitude attribution remains open. Current: 4xe production ended. 2026 refresh preserves V6 entry choice and adds turbo four above it; later SRT/refresh dates not firm.

**Customer.** For buyers who need two or three rows, towing, winter traction and a more premium cabin, GC/L competes with Explorer, Pilot/Telluride-class family SUVs and used premium SUVs. L expands utility but cannot be treated as entirely new demand. The turbo four adds torque and equipment on regular gasoline, while retained V6 reduces forced-engine-switch risk. A shopper without towing/off-road needs can obtain family space or hybrid economy elsewhere, including Cherokee. Used GC prices and trade-in equity compete with new payments. Former 4xe subsidized lessees may convert to ICE, competitors or delay; conversion is not 100%.

**Dealer.** A dealer can replenish broadly useful Laredo/Limited and L configurations if customer payment and fresh-stock turn cover acquisition, support and carrying cost. Old 4xe clearance is a separate decision: ended production and recall/repair status affect saleability and residual exposure. Simplifying trims can lower wrong-configuration inventory but cannot cure a too-high payment. Government-bid support demonstrates channel-specific economics, not retail margin. Summer stocking and elevated brand inventory mean a factory restart alone cannot justify unrestricted receipts.

**Competing evidence.** H1 2026 already about 203474 annualized; this supports a viable franchise despite Q3 weakness. Q3 NA shipment loss is not the same geography or population as US sales loss 16526. Some prior 4xe buyers were incentive-driven; a cheaper gasoline offer may retain them, so ended 4xe production is not a one-for-one family loss.

**Actions and status.** Implemented/current: refreshed equipment and turbo four offer, V6 entry retained; 4xe no longer produced. Management explanation: planned downtime; no actual daily plant-output series obtained. Planned: additional refresh/SRT during 2030 plan, no separately quantified 2028 uplift.

**Volume, price and mix.** Partial recovery anchored to H1 2026≈203k annualized and 2024 non-4xe 188558, not 2023 total 244594. Q4 scenarios 35/45/55k mean 11.7/15/18.3k per month versus Q3 12.7k and H1 17.0k; the upper end needs restored sellable supply. 2027/28 base 200/200k retains most H1 scale without an unsupported additional 2028 growth increment, downside persistent value/quality friction, upside strong refill and retention. No fitted seasonality. Base volume requires competitive financing and affordable configuration availability; lower 4xe share and Laredo mix can lower family average invoice before incentives. Rich L/Summit or SRT mix is an upside, not an automatic offset. No verified OEM realized dollars/unit; no positive price assumption solely from refresh.

**Revision and test.** Ended 4xe production and Cherokee overlap strengthen the need for gasoline retention; base trimmed modestly. These are judgment revisions, not measured unit losses attributable to cessation. Two quarters above ≈17k monthly at stable effective payments and acceptable fresh-stock turns support upside; continued ≈13k monthly despite available Laredo/Limited supply rejects base. Need sales by powertrain/channel and delivered configuration, not another factory announcement. Confidence: Medium mechanism / low-to-medium forecast.

Evidence: J01, J02, J03, J05, J07, J09, J10, J11, J14, J15, J22.

### Wrangler including former 4xe

**View.** An already recovering differentiated franchise; defend about current pace rather than restore 2018–19 volumes. Observed 2026 growth coexists with now-ended 4xe production; the undated cessation notice and remaining dealer stock do not establish the sequence or prove post-exit retention.

**Historical sequence.** 2018–19: 240032/228032; JL transition-era scale precedes Bronco re-entry and later high-price mix. 2022–24: 181409/156581/151163; 4xe and trims changed the proposition while direct off-road competition broadened. No causal lost-unit split established. 2025: 167322, already+10.7%; 2026 YTD 131011, +2.3%, Q3 44757. Recovery is not a future initiative to add again. Current: 4xe production ended; 2026 monthly editions/performance variants defend interest within the same family.

**Customer.** Open-air body, trail capability, personalization and enthusiast identity distinguish Wrangler from ordinary crossovers. Bronco is the direct new-vehicle alternative; used Wranglers can supply similar recreation at lower upfront cost, while practical SUVs win on daily comfort and payments. The discontinued 4xe offer removes the subsidized electric-commute path; gasoline familiarity and engine choice can retain enthusiasts but do not replicate every former lease payment. Edition demand may be substitution from standard trims.

**Dealer.** Dealers benefit from a recognizable enthusiast franchise and targeted configurations, but expensive customized stock ties up capital when buyers negotiate against used vehicles and Bronco. Reordering requires actual contribution after finance/cash support, not headline MSRP premium. 4xe residual stock and older recalled vehicles must be tracked separately from new gasoline replenishment. Accessory/service opportunities are plausible but not quantified.

**Competing evidence.** 2026 YTD growth argues against broad nameplate rejection. Exact production-end timing and old-stock sell-through overlap are unresolved, so it does not establish growth after the exit. June 2026 steering recall concerns vehicles built through Dec 2024; it does not establish a 2026 assembly shortage. No matched Bronco versus Wrangler current payments or national order-profit panel recovered.

**Actions and status.** Current: gasoline lineup and limited editions; 4xe production ended. Recall: battery issue specific to PHEV; separate steering connection campaign covers older Wrangler/Gladiator. Heritage redesign/Scrambler and SRT plans lack a verified incremental 2028 delivery calendar.

**Volume, price and mix.** Recent pace: 2026 YTD annualized 174681, Q3 annualized 179028. Base 180k both years. Q4 37/43/48k surrounds recent 14.9k/month with seasonal caution: 2024/25 Q4 were 38.1/39.3k; no fixed multiplier fitted. Upside requires retention/conquest; downside payment or reputation erosion. Competitive cash/lease support remains necessary. Lower 4xe mix can reduce sticker average while also changing manufacturer support; net margin direction cannot be inferred. Limited editions can aid mix only at real turn, not stock accumulation.

**Revision and test.** Retain current-pace base; widen 2028 downside for competition and replacement timing. Do not add a 4xe recovery layer. Base fails if normal available gasoline stock cannot sustain ≈15k/month without increasing support/age; upside requires durable conquest rather than edition mix or fleet batching. Confidence: Medium relative to other Jeep rows.

Evidence: J01, J05, J08, J09, J10, J16, J17.

### Gladiator

**View.** Treat as a smaller lifestyle-pickup franchise requiring competitive payments, not a broad midsize-truck share recovery.

**Historical sequence.** 2019 launch-year 40047 is partial-year and not mature capacity. 2023: 55188; 2024: 42123 after product/price/competitive pressure; no evidence assigns the decline to one cause. 2025: 56790 with unusually strong Q4 18277; 2026 Q1/Q2/Q3: 10087/8946/11911, YTD-19.7%.2025 rebound did not establish a durable annual level.

**Customer.** Open-air Jeep utility and pickup bed distinguish Gladiator for recreation; Wrangler substitutes when a bed is unnecessary. Tacoma, Ranger and Colorado are alternative utility purchases; discounted Ram 1500 can also compete at similar payment. Buyers prioritizing quiet highway use, fuel economy or work hauling may not pay a Jeep-lifestyle premium. Current advertised 3.6 V6/automatic and 19 mpg combined put fuel/payment costs into the choice; absence of a new powertrain is not evidence of rejection of an unavailable alternative.

**Dealer.** Expensive off-road trims need customer-specific stocking. Dealers can make an old-stock bargain attractive without that proving they can reorder fresh vehicles at the same net cost. The Idaho bid illustrates acquisition/support arithmetic: invoice 38479+destination 1995-government support 3000+1000 margin=38474; that 1000 is quoted gross markup before holding/selling costs, for government buyers only. A broad retail forecast cannot borrow it.

**Competing evidence.** Q3 sequential increase shows some demand; no reason to project permanent collapse. One government quote and unverified dealer website promotions cannot establish national replenishment margin. Recall on older production does not establish current new-unit stop-sale.

**Actions and status.** Current: automatic/V6 lineup and limited editions. Refresh/redesign within 2030 plan, undated; no independent 2027–28 lift assigned. Possible Dakota arrival in 2028 increases internal midsize-pickup overlap; no percentage cannibalization invented.

**Volume, price and mix.** Q3 12k scale and 2026 YTD annualized 41.3k support 43k then 45k bases. Q4 9/12/17k spans recent pace and highly variable prior Q4; upside reaches 55/60k only with effective value and repeat orders. Downside 2028 reduced to 30k for potential internal launch overlap. Base needs continued affordability support; a volume rebound with richer trims and fewer discounts simultaneously is unproven. Lower-priced variants or sustained support could lift units but must be charged once to net realization/cost.

**Revision and test.** Current-pace base retained; 2025 Q4 not annualized as sustainable. Broader 2028 downside reflects internal pickup competition. Fresh model-year Gladiators repeatedly selling at ≈4k/month with unchanged support would justify higher base; strong retail discounts plus continued age accumulation reject profitable recovery. Confidence: Medium-low.

Evidence: J01, J05, J08, J14, J16, J17.

### Compass

**View.** Partial recovery is possible, but summer downtime cannot explain the whole decline; this is also a continuing affordability, availability and portfolio-positioning problem.

**Historical sequence.** 2018–19: 171167/143934; pre-pandemic erosion already existed. 2022–24: 86175/96173/111697: growth during a period of wider Jeep contraction, so not every unit is a recoverable shortage. 2025: 101997.2026 Q1-41.5%, Q2-20.1%, Q3-61.5%; H1 lost 17808 before Q3 planned downtime. 2026 YTD 47837, Q3 10811. Returning Cherokee adds a larger hybrid choice above Compass and potential internal substitution.

**Customer.** Compact size, AWD/off-road image and a lower ticket than Cherokee/GC provide a role. Budget shoppers can instead buy a Trax/Envista-class lower-cost crossover, used SUV or mainstream compact. Those products are not capability equivalents, but buyers who rarely use Jeep capability may prefer space/equipment/payment. A Cherokee hybrid can look better for fuel economy and room if the payment gap narrows. The new European Compass is not a verified near-term US product improvement.

**Dealer.** Dealers need a meaningful payment step below Cherokee and sufficient desirable entry inventory; a costly well-optioned Compass next to a more efficient Cherokee is harder to stock. Planned supply reductions can protect dealer capital, but weak retail can also cause them. A subsequent factory restart adds value only if fresh units turn at an economic price. Fleet contract lists some 2026 Compass variants as limited availability, but that is order/bid language, not a national shortage count.

**Competing evidence.** 2024 sales grew 16.1%; the family retained customers when product/offer fit worked. H1 weakness disproves Q3 downtime as a sufficient explanation of YTD decline, but not a contribution in Q3. High brand days supply can coexist with shortages of specific affordable Compass configurations.

**Actions and status.** Current: existing US product; company cites Q3 planned downtime. Dealer estimated model-year dates do not independently establish restart or volume. North American next-generation timing not established; do not import Europe launch date.

**Volume, price and mix.** Q4 9/15/21k implies 3/5/7k monthly versus Q3 3.6k and H1 6.2k. Base 65/70k in 2027/28 is a partial normalization near 2026 H1 annualized 74k, materially below 2024/25; downside continued erosion, upside fuller supply with a viable payment gap. Portfolio-check jointly with Cherokee. Base needs better affordable availability and price separation from Cherokee. Lower mix and/or continuing support are likely conditions of volume, not a simultaneous margin-free gain. No national incentive estimate available.

**Revision and test.** Base retained as partial recovery, not accepted factory-only diagnosis; 2028 downside widened for aging/internal substitution. Base requires ≈5.4k/month 2027 with stable dealer economics. Available stock still selling ≈3.6k/month would reject normalization; confirmed new US generation/low-price availability could lift upside. Confidence: Low-to-medium.

Evidence: J01, J05, J06, J07, J08, J14, J15, J22.

### Cherokee / returning HEV

**View.** A real segment re-entry with useful hybrid utility; profitable affordable configurations and dealer conversion, rather than nameplate nostalgia, determine scale.

**Historical sequence.** 2018: 239437; 2019: 191397. Pre-chip inventory imbalance and capacity adjustment already present. 2021 chips interrupted production; 2021 relative listing turn was slow. 2022/23 cheaper-configuration availability narrowed, then production ended. Do not claim chips caused trim withdrawal. 2023: 24609, 2024: 2839, 2025: 527 runoff. New HEV launches into a product gap; no automatic restoration of old 191k. 2026 HEV Q3 10344 units, YTD 20914. Q2 raw table distinguishes old Cherokee 11 from HEV 8101; latest labels/vintages differ. Launch promised early 2026 lower trims; July 2026 management still restricts trims/channels for landed economics and promises competitive trims H1 2027.

**Customer.** Hybrid economy without plugging in, standard AWD and useful family space suit buyers choosing RAV4/CR-V/Forester/Sportage hybrids, or upgrading from Compass. Independent comparison finds a competitive drivetrain; Subaru offers more standard comfort features and better utility/value at a slightly lower entry price. Cherokee must win an actual equipped payment, not merely advertise 35000 before destination. Buyers can stay with an older Jeep or purchase used; a remembered brand name does not erase a multiyear absence.

**Dealer.** Dealers need enough affordable sellable trims to convert inquiries and replenish them without uneconomic acquisition cost. Toluca tariffs and limited channels can make a theoretically popular entry price impossible to supply profitably. Trailhawk may offer attractive gross dollars to enthusiasts but does not solve the entry-price problem. Initial allocation and inquiry counts are weaker than repeat fresh unit orders. Government catalog availability cannot override July management testimony about economics.

**Competing evidence.** Actual sales ramp from Q1 to Q3 and favorable drivetrain comparison support genuine demand; no zero-demand inference. Forester comparison favors competitor overall, limiting confident conquest assumptions. Original early 2026 availability promise versus July economic restrictions shows announcement is not broad availability.

**Actions and status.** Delivered: HEV sales; primary launch offer 36995 including destination. Orders: Trailhawk at 44625 including destination; production planned later 2026. Planned: more competitive trims H1 2027; localization date unspecified in July call. No automatic 2028 cost localization benefit.

**Volume, price and mix.** Constrained ramp: Q4 9/13/17k; base 4.3k/month versus Q3 3.45k, before promised H1 2027 value actions. 2027 35/55/75k and 2028 40/70/100k require base 4.6k then 5.8k monthly, not old 191k. Downside trims stay uneconomic; upside timely broad offer and conquest. No mechanically borrowed Trax ramp. Volume acceleration must accompany lower average trim/payment or cost improvement. Lower-priced trims can dilute family price while adding contribution; tariff/production savings spent on affordability cannot also be fully retained in AOI. No verified OEM landed cost or net revenue per unit.

**Revision and test.** Modestly lower base for partial-year competitive-trim availability and concrete rival value; still well above current pace if constraints ease. No measured elasticity implied. Profitable delivered entry/Laredo inventory plus ≈5k monthly through 2027 supports base; persistent ≈3.5k despite inventory, rising incentives or delayed H1 2027 offer rejects it. Need actual trim deliveries and earned dealer support, not website starting price. Confidence: Medium product mechanism / low forecast.

Evidence: J01, J04, J05, J06, J08, J12, J13, J14, J21, J22.

### Wagoneer / Grand Wagoneer combined

**View.** Hold a rebuilt full-size-SUV base; naming consolidation and more accessible Grand entry cannot justify luxury-mix uplift by themselves.

**Historical sequence.** Absent in 2018/19; current franchise launched 2021/2022 into full-size three-row luxury/utility segment. 2023 combined 39767; 2024 rose to 55084 while Jeep weakened; 2025 fell to 45040. Growth was not a universal brand recovery. 2026 Q1/Q2/Q3: 15242/14905/12495. YTD 42642, +23%, but Q3-30%; early growth and later pullback coexist. Wagoneer/Grand Wagoneer naming now combined in sales. 2026 Grand current offer begins 63995; retain one stable family.

**Customer.** Large families and towing buyers need third-row space plus cargo and towing together; Expedition/Tahoe/Yukon and used full-size SUVs are direct alternatives. Premium appointments can compete higher in price, but a Grand badge does not make every sale a luxury transaction. The 420 hp engine and advertised 10000 lb tow support a specific utility proposition; buyers not requiring it can choose GCL or a minivan at lower running costs.

**Dealer.** High acquisition dollars make time in stock costly. A simplified family and more accessible entry offer can broaden turn, but dealer willingness needs repeat sales with net gross after support, not a cheap older unit sold once. Government assistance shows channel support is meaningful and differs from other Jeeps; national retail gross remains unknown. High-end Summit stock must be justified separately from volume entry configurations.

**Competing evidence.** 2024 was already a growth year; restoring 2024 scale is less speculative than assuming steady share gains. 2026 YTD pace ≈56.9k supports 55k base, but Q3 pace ≈50k and high-brand stock limit optimism. No clean channel/trim series establishes that refresh, renaming or price caused growth.

**Actions and status.** Current: refreshed 2026 family and broad Grand trim ladder. Planned: REEV/SRT and later refresh within broader strategy; family platform dates are not verified customer-delivery dates.

**Volume, price and mix.** Current pace 55k then 58k base, not two additive nameplates. Q4 10/14/18k brackets recent 4.2–5.0k monthly and variable prior Q4. Upside 65/70k needs sustained conquest and economics, downside 40k reverts toward pre-recovery scale. Lower entry configurations may support volume while diluting former Grand-name mix. Forecast combined family price; no apparent mix upgrade solely from transferring Wagoneer labels. Premium and REEV upside needs actual transactions and costs.

**Revision and test.** Base retained because latest annualized selling scale supports it; lower entry/mix conditions now explicit. ≈4.6–4.8k monthly at stable support and inventory age supports base. Declining fresh-stock turn despite revised offer rejects recovery; sustained high end transactions, not naming, establish favorable mix. Confidence: Medium-low.

Evidence: J01, J05, J08, J14, J18.

### Wagoneer S BEV

**View.** An interrupted, expensive launch with product and payment constraints; evidence does not support either a mature zero-demand verdict or an automatic 2027 recovery.

**Historical sequence.** 2024 reported 231; 2025 10864, of which Q3 4163 and Q4 only 438. 2026 Q1/Q2/Q3: 175/198/146; YTD 519. April 2026 report conveys OEM decision to skip MY2026, improve battery/software/capability and add NACS for 2027. Current MY2025 stock continued to be advertised.

**Customer.** Premium electric performance competes with Model Y, Mach-E, EV6/Ioniq 5 and premium used EVs; it does not supply full-size Wagoneer seating/towing just because of the name. NACS can improve charging convenience but does not alone fix purchase payment, software confidence, service or depreciation. Existing stock means low sales are not explained solely by absent production; exact sellable quantity/trim/payment remains unknown.

**Dealer.** Aged EV inventory needs clearance economics and software/remedy readiness. A dealer should reorder a 2027 revision only after improved specification and payment produce repeat fresh sales; production restart is not an order-profit signal. April promotional maxima are neither October offers nor inferred manufacturer net-price loss.

**Competing evidence.** 2025 >10k proves some sales at prior offer/policy conditions; not proof of profitable mature demand. Skipped model year and unknown restart restrict interpretation of 2026 units; old stock remains a counterweight to a pure supply-shortage explanation.

**Actions and status.** Reported OEM plan: skip 2026 MY, return 2027 with NACS and improvements; precise production/dealer timing unverified.

**Volume, price and mix.** Leave annual and Q4 units unallocated pending dated restart, supply and payment evidence. Tiny Q3 pace is not mature potential; no fabricated ramp. Likely support-intensive relaunch; do not use MSRP as net realization. No baseline high-margin EV mix contribution.

**Revision and test.** Status is now an explicitly interrupted program, not merely missing research. Confirmed broad 2027 deliveries and several economically viable reorder cycles can admit a ramp; continued old-stock discounting without new configuration supply cannot. Confidence: Low.

Evidence: J01, J05, J19.

### Recon BEV / possible later ICE

**View.** Early limited supply in a differentiated niche; demand and timing are too unobserved for a defensible numeric ramp.

**Historical sequence.** 2025 reported 56; 2026 YTD 82 including Q3 35. These tiny sales are not evidence of a mature retail rollout. May 2026 plan names BEV and eventual ICE. Current official page advertises limited quantities and 66995 starting MSRP.

**Customer.** Open-air electric off-roading differentiates it from conventional electric crossovers but narrows the audience at its price. Wrangler is an internal choice for off-road identity without charging needs; Rivian and premium EVs compete for adventurous buyers. Charging away from cities, range and service matter as much as horsepower. No direct comparable justifies extrapolating a successful launch curve.

**Dealer.** Dealers need saleable demonstrators, service readiness and visible local interest before taking costly EV stock. Small early allocations may be marketing or constrained availability, not evidence of voluntary scaled replenishment.

**Competing evidence.** Limited availability is explicit; tiny units cannot prove permanent consumer rejection. Distinct capability is not evidence of sufficient buyers at the advertised price.

**Actions and status.** Current marketed BEV, limited quantities. ICE announced within plan but date unverified.

**Volume, price and mix.** Unallocated through 2028 until production/delivered-stock and payment evidence establish a ramp; do not silently treat as zero potential. Premium sticker and potential finance support; no margin or transaction calibration. BEV/ICE must share one family; Wrangler substitution belongs in portfolio scenarios.

**Revision and test.** Current official offer sharpens positioning and supply limitation but not unit sizing. Broad available dealer stock, sustained qualified conversions and voluntary orders would permit numerical ramp; absence of these is uncertainty, not cancellation. Confidence: Low.

Evidence: J01, J08, J20.

### Jeep discontinued runoff / Renegade

**View.** Runoff should fade, with unlisted legacy sales retained in reconciliation rather than falsely set to zero.

**Historical sequence.** Renegade 2018/19: 97062/76885; 2023: 26011; 2024: 8440; 2025: 721. 2026 Q1/Q2: 23/10; no separate Q3 row.

**Customer.** Remaining inexpensive inventory may attract price-led buyers; this does not establish ongoing factory replenishment or a new US entry product.

**Dealer.** Old-stock clearance can release capital; no recurring acquisition decision after cessation. Net discounts and age are transaction-specific.

**Competing evidence.** A missing Q3 named row is not zero sales; Jeep brand residual must remain.

**Actions and status.** Runoff; no verified new US replacement with 2028 individual milestones in this pass.

**Volume, price and mix.** No standalone forecast assigned; preserve Jeep other/unlisted residual. Operational runoff approaches zero, but numeric zero is not an observed value. Clearance may be heavily discounted but is financially immaterial at current units; do not apply discount rate nationally.

**Revision and test.** Explicit scope/residual treatment. A newly dated US product or material reported sales changes treatment. Confidence: High direction / low exact units.

Evidence: J01, J08.

## Reconciliation and model admission

The Q3 eight named Jeep rows total 128526 versus brand 128542, leaving **16 unlisted units**. Latest YTD named rows total 413713 versus 413793, leaving **80**. Keep those residuals. Earlier published Cherokee-family Q1+Q2+Q3 sum 20945 versus latest YTD 20914: **−31 reporting-vintage/label difference**, not demand. Do not force old quarters to sum the newest YTD.

The 2028 base Grand Cherokee+Compass+Cherokee is 345k versus 312.6k in 2025 and 330.7k in 2024. This constrains internal overlap but does not measure customer-level cannibalization. New trims, SRT, L and powertrain derivatives stay within families. Current family growth already achieved is not added again as a launch uplift.

Observed sales/status can enter the evidence ledger. Rounded scenarios can enter a proposed assumption table with their pricing conditions. OEM net revenue/unit, dealer contribution and independent retail-only demand remain unobserved. US customer units cannot be directly multiplied by NA wholesale revenue/unit without the architecture’s shipment and price/mix reconciliation.

## Bounded method and remaining gaps

Recovered the architecture, complete Jeep volume rows, May launch register, dealer/household work and earlier GC/Cherokee/comparative investigations before six targeted discovery queries. Follow-ups pursued concrete current status, original recall, official product and operator-contract leads. This route is stronger for identifying what is deliverable and who bears cost than collecting unverified discount screenshots. It is insufficient for national payment elasticity or reorder profitability; no bulk collection, paid data, agent spawning, workbook edit or invented causal fit was performed.

The highest-value unresolved observations are: actual GC/Compass production and fresh affordable stock around the shutdown; delivered Cherokee lower trim economics and H1 2027 milestones; matched dealer receipts/retail/earned support through several repeat orders; and firm Wagoneer S/Recon broad-delivery timing. Existing historical lender and Cherokee trim-causality dead ends were not repeated. A material data purchase or inventory scrape should be evaluated separately.
