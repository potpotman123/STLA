# Model causal audit and Grand Cherokee revision

14 October 2026 · D096. US customer sales include retail and fleet. Existing dossiers were recovered before eight targeted queries and selective primary-source checks. This is a complete coverage audit, not a claim that every causal question has been resolved. No agents, bulk collection or causal fitting were used.

## Grand Cherokee: revised stabilization case

Reduce both 2027 and 2028 from 200,000 to **190,000**. Revising only 2028 would leave the unsupported H1 restoration in 2027. The revised case requires 15,833 monthly sales, near 2026 YTD annualized sales of 186,352 and below trailing-year sales of 195,625. It assumes some recovery from disrupted Q3 conditions, but no full restoration of H1's pace or additional structural growth.

This is a rounded scenario judgment, not an estimated demand coefficient. The 200,000 level remains a conditional recovery reference; the formal downside/upside cases remain unchanged. The quantified portfolio totals become 1,381,150 in 2027 and 1,416,000 in 2028. These exclude unallocated launches and are customer sales, not North American shipments. The five core families total 980,000 in 2028.

## Wrangler: gasoline resilience beneath the plug-in decline

Sales declined from 228,032 in 2019 to 156,581 in 2023 and 151,163 in 2024, then recovered to 167,322 in 2025. Competition, affordability and product mix matter; differentiation does not guarantee restoration of historical share.

The 2023–24 decomposition is revealing: 4xe fell from 67,429 to 55,554 (−11,875), while other variants increased from 89,152 to 95,609 (+6,457, or 7.2%). The net decline was 5,418. This was not uniform rejection of the gasoline vehicle. Earlier allocation, financing and recall evidence supplies competing mechanisms; arithmetic cannot select their causal weights. Bronco competition cannot explain every variant and period on its own.

Retain 180,000 provisionally: its required 15,000 monthly pace is close to Q3 2026. This is supported by observed recovery, not simply the announcement of a corrective action. Current 4xe exit timing, retention and inventory-specific incentives remain risks. Sustainable volume must be consistent with the cost of supporting payments. Sources C1–C2 and the prior Jeep dossier.

## Chrysler Pacifica/Voyager: a resilient business with an affordable retail option

Combined sales were 120,554 in 2023, 119,389 in 2024 and 125,798 in 2025. There was no large 2024 collapse to reverse. Voyager's return to retail for MY2025 restored a lower-priced option with useful minivan equipment; orders opened in September 2024, with Q4 arrivals planned [C3]. This is a concrete mechanism for household conversion, but fleet deliveries, segment recovery and substitution from Pacifica remain alternative explanations of growth.

Retain 140,000: trailing-year sales of 137,236 provide a stronger anchor than a speculative turnaround. Keep Voyager and Pacifica combined through the LX naming change. Hybrid competitors and the PHEV exit create risks. Do not add a separate refresh uplift or infer household growth from rental deliveries.

## Gladiator: improved value, with inconsistent sales outcomes

Sales were 55,188 in 2023, 42,123 in 2024 and 56,790 in 2025; the 2026 pace subsequently weakened. In February 2024 Jeep cut the starting MSRP by $1,725 while adding screen and safety equipment [C4]. Sales still declined that year: a corrective action does not establish its success. MY2025 standard automatic transmission and revised pricing further changed the value proposition [C5]. Compare equivalent equipment and existing-stock discounts, not just base stickers.

Retain 43,000/45,000 for 2027/28, based on the weaker recent pace. Off-road and open-air utility differentiates the truck, but price and daily-use tradeoffs limit its audience. Manual-transmission recall evidence does not explain the whole loss without take rates. Repeat fresh-stock sales are needed before extrapolating the 2025 rebound; future Ram trucks may also substitute.

## Compass: shutdown timing does not explain the full problem

Sales grew from 96,173 in 2023 to 111,697 in 2024 before falling to 101,997 in 2025. H1 2026 lost 17,808 units before Q3 downtime. A supply-only explanation therefore fails the timing test.

The 65,000/70,000 forecast remains conditional. Compass needs an attractive payment below Cherokee and desirable entry configurations. Returning Cherokee and lower-priced competing crossovers can challenge its positioning. The European next generation is not evidence of a US launch. A sufficiently attractive payment gap has not been demonstrated; this is a material remaining forecast risk.

## Smaller Cherokee and Renegade: weakness plus replacement gaps

Cherokee fell from 239,437 in 2018 to 191,397 in 2019 before the chip shortage. Later disruptions, narrower configurations and discontinuation compounded the loss. We have not proved that chips caused the trim withdrawal. Renegade also disappeared without an immediate equivalent replacement.

The new Cherokee's 55,000/70,000 scenario depends on affordable trims with viable landed costs, rather than recovering its historical peak. July 2026 management commentary still described restricted trims/channels and planned H1 2027 value actions. Initial deliveries prove that a product exists, not its mature scale. Trailhawk does not solve entry affordability. Renegade remains runoff.

## Wagoneer family: a new franchise with volatile volume

Combined sales increased from 39,767 in 2023 to 55,084 in 2024, then fell to 45,040 in 2025. Strong H1 2026 and weaker Q3 coexist. Large-family, cargo and towing needs support a real customer proposition; expensive stock also creates substantial dealer holding costs.

Retain 55,000/58,000 provisionally. More accessible entry positioning and a simplified family could improve turnover, but need channel-adjusted repeat orders. Do not count renaming as growth or assume every Grand-badged sale improves premium mix. Future derivatives remain inside the family forecast rather than receiving duplicate launch credit.

## Dodge Charger/Challenger: a replacement gap and a different offer

The old combined family fell from 120,858 in 2023 to 61,810 in 2024. Charger alone sold 9,562 in 2025. Ending production before broad replacement availability is a tangible mechanism. But BEV and subsequent SIXPACK products change engine identity, pricing and customer appeal; restarting production does not recreate the old buyer proposition.

Retain 20,000/25,000 as explicit launch scenarios above Q3 2026 annualized sales of 14,716. Gasoline availability addresses part of the gap, while the higher entry price and used HEMI alternatives constrain restoration. Net ICE gains against BEV losses; do not assume a street HEMI launch or return to the combined 2019 peak.

## Durango: recovery already occurred

Sales of 69,196 in 2023 and 59,357 in 2024 rose to 81,168 in 2025, above 2019's 67,599. Seating, towing and HEMI character provide differentiation. The 2025 retail improvement preceded the 2026 R/T392 action, so the newer product cannot explain the earlier recovery.

Retain 90,000 provisionally, but recognize that it holds Q3's approximately 91,000 annualized pace rather than the lower YTD pace of approximately 82,000. Better performance content can improve conversion while transferring value to customers and pressuring old-stock margins. Do not award another large recovery for losses already reversed.

## Hornet: poor stock turnover before the production pause

Launch sales increased from 9,336 in 2023 to 20,559 in 2024, then fell to 9,365 in 2025. Prior marketplace evidence showed weak stock-flow balance before the pause. Performance and AWD at a higher entry price did not necessarily match mainstream compact-crossover buyers' priorities. Trax/Envista are useful value comparisons, not identical products or proof of causation.

The official indefinite production pause prevents normal replenishment. Retain 100/0 runoff conditional on no restart. Clearance transactions are not proof of a sustainable franchise. A GLH successor remains unallocated rather than being added to Hornet as independent growth. The precise economic cause of the pause remains unresolved.

## Ram HD and ProMaster: distinguish commercial cycles from LD recovery

HD's 170,000 case is near 2025 and trailing-year levels. The refresh and serviceability changes are tangible, but actual downtime and total ownership cost benefits are not measured. Sustained commercial replacement orders and completed fresh sales matter more than selective high-trim clearance.

ProMaster's 81,663 peak in 2023 fell to 65,869 in 2024 and 57,591 in 2025. The issuer still called 2024 its second-best year [C2]. Fleet batches, post-shortage catch-up and commercial demand are competing explanations; their weights remain unresolved. Retain 60,000 as stabilization near recent sales, not restoration of the peak. Orderability and upfit lead times do not prove final customer acceptance. No separate EV uplift is established.

## New launches and small residuals

ProMaster City at 10,000/15,000 requires planned delivery timing, upfit readiness and repeat commercial orders; potential substitution from the larger van matters. Dakota, compact Ram, Ram SUV, Chrysler crossovers, Recon and Wagoneer S remain unallocated where delivery dates or demand evidence cannot support a numerical ramp. Unallocated does not mean zero potential. Fiat/Alfa's 3,000 convention reflects its small recent footprint, not a demonstrated turnaround. Keep legacy runoff separate from new launches.

## Decision and provenance

Only Grand Cherokee's 2027/28 base volumes changed. This audit does not automatically validate the remaining forecasts. Compass recovery, Cherokee/City/Charger ramps and Durango's latest selling pace remain particularly conditional. Minivans have a comparatively strong maintenance anchor; Wrangler shows gasoline resilience but uncertain powertrain retention.
