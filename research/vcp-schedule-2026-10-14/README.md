## Why these rates, and why they may fail

1. **Components, 9%:** Nissan reported a 9% FY2001 purchasing reduction and JPY245bn accounting benefit. Carry this single observed step over the STLA 2026–28 program, without compounding it annually. Its cumulative 20% over two implementation years is the same turnaround and remains an alternative, not additive. Supplier rationalization, global sourcing and engineering changes match the broad mechanism, but Nissan's starting position and purchasing perimeter differ. Assuming transfer of the whole 9% to component spend is generous and not independently validated. Do not reinstate the rejected arbitrary 3% discount. Renault EUR400/vehicle covers wider variable costs and is only a cross-check, not extra component savings. [Nissan FY2001 business review](https://www.nissan-global.com/EN/DOCUMENT/HTML/FINANCIAL/SPEECH/2002/fs_speech20020509.html), May 9, 2002, financial-performance item 1 and NRP overview.

2. **Manufacturing, 8.3%:** Nissan reported this direct manufacturing cost-per-unit reduction over the three-year NISSAN 180 program, versus 14% including indirect costs. Use one cumulative reduction across the 2026–28 window, not 8.3% per year. Supplier synchronization, lead-time reduction, overtime and resource efficiency are credible mechanisms. Paid headcount/cash spending must actually fall; better OPE alone does not suffice. Cost-definition and absorption differences remain unresolved. Map EUR456.5m to labor and EUR124.5m to cash plant only as arithmetic allocations, not separate empirical findings. No depreciation saving. [Nissan annual report 2004](https://www.annualreports.com/HostedData/AnnualReportArchive/n/OTC_NSANY_2004.pdf), printed p51/PDF p53; [existing detailed benchmark note](../investor-day-2026-05-21/vcp-other-three-outcome-benchmarks.md).

3. **Logistics, 17%:** 4flow reports achieved reduction on optimized low-volume inbound transport through fuller loads and rescheduling. Use the observed 17%, not a made-up haircut, but explicitly assume equivalent network-wide opportunity. This is the largest scope leap in this row: a selected project does not prove every freight lane has that headroom. The reported two months describes software integration, not financial realization across the network. No rate is transferred to the separate EUR1bn indirect pool. [4flow case](https://www.4flow.com/supply-chain-software/integrated-material-and-transportation-planning-software/case-study-ltl).

4. **Warranty:** 3MIS provides a plausible mechanism, not a lifetime expense rate. Severity, older cohorts, coverage expansion and reserve revisions can offset early-claim improvements. Retained 2025 remeasurement nonrecurrence belongs in an explicitly identified non-VCP normalization line. No 5–10% forecast resurrected. Leave benefit uncredited in this working case and visibly unestimated in the evidence register. This is a material open issue, not a completed four-driver causal forecast.

## Implementation and controls

- Keep frozen reference pools; no automatic sales-growth multiplier. This is a comparable-output program scenario, not a forecast of total material or manufacturing expense.
- Effective annual rates for components: 0.9%, 3.6%, 9%; manufacturing: 0.83%, 3.32%, 8.3%; logistics: 1.7%, 6.8%, 17%. Those rates already contain timing: do not apply timing factors a second time.
- Preserve the four workstreams. Either show logistics EUR1.5bn plus a visibly unestimated EUR1bn indirect support row, or use combined EUR2.5bn with effective credited rates 1.02%, 4.08%, 10.2%. Never apply 17% to EUR2.5bn.
- Enter 0 credited for warranty and indirect only with a visible note that underlying savings remain unestimated. The machine-readable reference rate is null, not a claimed measured zero.
- Add only current-year minus prior-year recognized savings to the AOI bridge. Use the selected EUR5,000 volume/mix coefficient separately. Do not add another utilization allowance inside VCP; Nissan's manufacturing benchmark may contain absorption, so this remains a transfer-risk flag rather than a verified clean productivity measure.
- Exclude freight embedded in purchased components, factory services already in cash manufacturing and scrap/rework already elsewhere. Nominally separate pools do not independently establish perfect scope reconciliation.
- Numbers are before separately modeled commodity/wage inflation, tariffs, pricing giveback, launch spending and incremental implementation expenses. Execution expense is not quantified here; do not label the subtotal net of all costs or the full AOI recovery. Add such costs once, in the appropriate industrial/SG&A or adjustment line; do not invent a hidden haircut.
- No savings attributed to SG&A from unestimated indirect procurement. Keep modeled credited subtotal in industrial; do not assume underlying program accounting is all industrial if future evidence allocates otherwise.

## What this establishes

The benchmark-transfer case is approximately EUR4bn of quantified NA benefit, with warranty/indirect not credited. It does **not** support a claim that management's more-than-EUR3bn NA ambition is impossible. It also does not prove that ambition achievable: component transfer, freight scope and recognition timing dominate the uncertainty. Selection of these rates is judgment; historical outcomes and management timing are evidence. A genuinely skeptical thesis must explain specific failures or offsets, not force lower percentages to reach a desired conclusion.
