# Is a 9% cumulative purchased-component reduction plausible?

14 October 2026. Bounded follow-up to the user's challenge. Conclusion: **plausible but ambitious as a gross, comparable-content 2025-to-2028 reduction; not validated as a full-pool net saving or an incremental benefit on top of ordinary purchasing productivity**. No rate or workbook changed.

## Stronger evidence from suppliers

- **Motherson 2024 placement document:** issuer-hosted indexed passage says OEMs agree price reductions annually under long-term contracts; typical average effective product-price reduction is 2–3% per unit, **excluding customer-directed components**. This is closer to purchased-part economics than general OEM restructuring. Source: https://www.motherson.com/storage/Corporate%20Announcements/FY2024-25/Final_PD.21.09.24.pdf . Access limitation: exact passage retrieved through web search index; direct PDF returned 403, so no verified page locator or complete surrounding document review. An older 2016 issuer document contains similar annual 2–3% language, but it is not counted as an independent observation: https://www.motherson.com/storage/list-directory-items/289_1_PlacementDocument.pdf . Direct older PDF also failed to open. Do not represent the indexed disclosure as a representative industry survey.
- **Aptiv SEC correspondence, 2024:** annual per-part reductions are customary at program award. Offered targets are not binding; actual amounts can change with volumes/costs and may depend on joint cost savings and negotiations. Supports the recurring mechanism and simultaneously limits certainty. https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1521332/000152133224000043/filename1.htm , response on revenue recognition / APR, pp3–4.
- **Lear FY2024 10-K:** contracts generally include annual price reductions over a vehicle's production life; product content/costs and other commercial factors can change prices. Corroborates mechanism, not Motherson's percentage. https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/842162/000084216225000010/lear-20241231.htm , customer-imposed price reduction risk.

## Scale arithmetic, not independent validation

- 9% total reduction over three annual cycles requires 1 - 0.91^(1/3) = **3.095% per year**.
- Repeated 2% yields **5.881%** cumulative; repeated 3% yields **8.733%**.
- If only 2027–28 are available, the required equivalent annual rate is **4.606%**, materially harder.
- These calculations hold comparable content and eligible spend constant. Motherson's exclusion means its 2–3% cannot simply be assigned to every euro of STLA's EUR35bn pool. New products, tooling, commodity clauses, coverage and price resets matter.
- This is a plausibility check on terminal scale, not a new annual schedule. Our proposed late-heavy 2026–28 recognition path remains more demanding in 2028 than a smooth 3.1% annual reduction; do not claim this arithmetic validates that path.

## STLA implementation evidence

A July 8, 2026 Auburn Hills VCP Technical Operations Manager posting explicitly identifies the B1 Direct Material Cost Workstream and work on current production vehicles. Responsibilities include supplier/engineering workshops, component/system assessments, challenging material selections and manufacturing methods, teardowns, validation and implementation. This supports that the proposed engineering mechanism has an operational role, not merely broad factory cost cutting. A job posting demonstrates intended activity, not that the position is filled, savings realized or quantified. https://careers.stellantis.com/job/23577929/vcp-technical-operations-manager/ .

Nissan FY2011 supplies a post-turnaround cross-check: JPY200.1bn purchasing savings were separately disclosed from JPY115.6bn energy/raw-material headwinds. No matched procurement denominator established, so do not derive a percentage. It also illustrates why gross purchasing performance is not net margin improvement. https://www.nissan-global.com/EN/DOCUMENT/HTML/FINANCIAL/SPEECH/2011/2011results_speech_154_e.html . Nissan's broader 5% annual monozukuri claim includes manufacturing/logistics/R&D and is excluded as a component-only rate. Renault's EUR400 variable-COGS result remains broader than components and is not used to validate 9%.

## Model/memo treatment

Retain 9% as an explicit ambitious benchmark-transfer assumption, now supported by recurring supplier concessions as well as Nissan's exceptional turnaround. Include ordinary annual supplier reductions **within** the 9%; never add them again as non-VCP productivity. Do not call 9% incremental versus a baseline already incorporating those concessions. Keep the cost reference at 2025 comparable volume/content, price/inflation effects separately identified, and 2026 embedded savings deducted before adding the 2027 YoY benefit. EUR3.15bn remains arithmetic on an estimated regional pool, not a reported opportunity.

Memo position: repeated OEM-negotiated part-price reductions make a high-single-digit cumulative reduction over three years economically plausible, supported by engineering optimization. Coverage, rollout timing and inflation prevent treating it as guaranteed, net of offsets or a demonstrated STLA-specific cost gap. The supplier evidence improves plausibility; it does not independently validate the forecast.
