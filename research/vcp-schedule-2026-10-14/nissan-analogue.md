# Nissan analogue: mechanism versus transferable percentage

14 October 2026. User challenged whether mature-company conditions make Nissan comparable. No numerical forecast changed. This qualifies the 9% component transfer in the current proposal; it does not validate it or justify an arbitrary haircut.

## Starting position

Nissan was established in 1933, not a startup: [issuer overview](https://www.nissan-global.com/EN/COMPANY/OVERVIEW/). Its FY2001 review reports 2.597m vehicles sold globally and 125,100 employees. It was a mature international OEM restructuring an inefficient organization, with established Japanese and US businesses. Global sales declined 1.4% in FY2001 even as purchasing savings improved profit: this was not simply startup scale or a booming sales denominator. [FY2001 review](https://www.nissan-global.com/EN/DOCUMENT/HTML/FINANCIAL/SPEECH/2002/fs_speech20020509.html).

## What changed

- Purchasing was centralized from country/regional operations into a global organization. Nine cross-functional international teams initially developed the recovery plan. This released existing group buying power rather than requiring an entirely new company. [1999 report, PDF p4](https://www.nissan-global.com/EN/IR/FINANCIAL_RESULTS/ASSETS/DATA/1999/ar1999.pdf).
- Parts suppliers fell 40% to 700 and service suppliers 60%; concentrating orders and sourcing globally were explicit purchasing levers. Do not assume supplier-count reduction equals percentage price reduction. [FY2001 review](https://www.nissan-global.com/EN/DOCUMENT/HTML/FINANCIAL/SPEECH/2002/fs_speech20020509.html).
- Nissan 3-3-3 brought engineering into purchasing, using more than 70,000 suggestions. The new Renault-Nissan purchasing organization covered about 30% of combined worldwide purchasing turnover at the time. Engineering and alliance purchasing are distinct from simply demanding supplier discounts. [2001 report, printed p29/PDF p31](https://www.nissan-global.com/EN/IR/FINANCIAL_RESULTS/ASSETS/DATA/2001/ar2001.pdf).
- Japan's car-production system went from seven plants/24 platforms to four/15. Management also reported broader capacity utilization rising from 51% to 75%. These have different perimeters and should not be combined into a single plant ratio. Plant closures/asset sales and workforce changes accompanied purchasing improvements; do not attribute their profit effects to the 9% purchasing metric. [2001 report](https://www.nissan-global.com/EN/IR/FINANCIAL_RESULTS/ASSETS/DATA/2001/ar2001.pdf), printed p27; FY2001 review.

## Comparison with Stellantis

Relevant similarities: mature brands and global operations; a turnaround under changed leadership; supplier negotiation plus engineering redesign; product/platform complexity; an underused manufacturing footprint. VCP's engineering, purchasing and finance teams and component benchmarking map particularly well to Nissan's technical-purchasing mechanism. [STLA financial overview, p2](https://www.stellantis.com/content/dam/stellantis-corporate/investors/events/stellantis-investor-day-2026/transcript/Stellantis-Investor-Day-2026-FaSTLAne2030-Transcript-Financial-Overview.pdf).

Material differences: Nissan was newly globalizing fragmented procurement and creating a new alliance purchasing organization. Stellantis already reported EUR8.4bn net cash synergies in 2023 after its merger; this is a global cash-synergy measure, not component-only NA AOI savings, and cannot be subtracted from VCP. It does establish previous significant integration activity. [2023 annual report](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1605484/000160548424000022/stellantis-20231231.htm). Remaining STLA supplier price gaps, part complexity and savings from redesign have not been quantified here. Global Nissan purchasing is also not identical to our estimated NA materials pool. Supplier capacity, product requirements and current trade arrangements differ.

Conclusion: strong precedent that a mature OEM can achieve large reductions through organizational and engineering changes; conditional analogue for STLA's mechanisms; weak calibration for an automatic 9% cut to the entire NA component base. The EUR3.15bn component estimate remains a generous benchmark-transfer scenario. Evidence needed to strengthen it: matched component quotes/teardowns, avoidable part variants and concrete redesign savings with launch dates, plus how much is incremental to prior synergy programs. Do not infer all remaining opportunity is exhausted merely because prior savings were large.

Program separation: the 9% purchasing result belongs to FY2001 within the Revival Plan. The 8.3% manufacturing outcome used elsewhere belongs to the subsequent three-year NISSAN 180 program. They are not simultaneous reductions from the same year.
