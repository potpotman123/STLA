# Manufacturing, logistics/indirect and warranty: plausibility update

14 October 2026. Bounded follow-up to the component 9% plausibility check. No workbook, rates or recognition schedule changed. All cost pools below remain estimated NA pools, not disclosed segment costs. Annual terminal benefits are relative to the frozen reference at comparable output/content, not annual incremental savings or cumulative cash savings.

| Driver | Existing assumption | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Manufacturing cash | EUR7bn × 8.3% = EUR581m | Plausible conditional historical transfer; cash realization and absorption overlap unresolved |
| Logistics | EUR1.5bn × 17% = EUR255m | Observed project outcome, aggressive whole-pool transfer |

## Manufacturing

[Nissan annual report 2004](https://www.annualreports.com/HostedData/AnnualReportArchive/n/OTC_NSANY_2004.pdf), printed p51, reports direct cost per unit down 8.3% during the three-year NISSAN180 program, and 14% including indirect costs. Described mechanisms include production/supplier sequencing and better placement of parts for assembly. This is distinct from FY2001 purchasing savings. The compounded equivalent of 8.3% over three years is approximately 2.85% annually; that is a scale check, not an implementation curve. Nissan's cost perimeter and utilization contribution are not demonstrated to match our cash pool.

[Renault, 7 December 2023](https://media.renaultgroup.com/with-re-industry-renault-group-is-launching-an-ambitious-plan-to-transform-its-industrial-base/?lang=fra), reports EUR270m savings in 2023 from its industrial digital system, primarily predictive maintenance. This supports maintenance/downtime mechanisms, but lacks a matching denominator for a transferable percentage. Correction to any earlier broad characterization: this release attributes the EUR270m primarily to maintenance, not logistics. Its prospective production-cost targets and capex reduction are not achieved AOI savings to add here.

For STLA, reduced downtime, overtime, rework, energy and maintenance expenditure can lower cash cost at the same output. More vehicles from unchanged resources can improve unit cost without the same cash saving: do not also count that volume absorption in VCP after crediting the volume/mix bridge. Improved OPE alone does not establish EUR581m realized savings.

## Logistics and indirect

[4flow case](https://www.4flow.com/supply-chain-software/integrated-material-and-transportation-planning-software/case-study-ltl): an anonymous OEM's low-volume inbound transport optimization delivered 17% transport cost savings and 74% higher load utilization through consolidation/rescheduling. The two-month reference concerns software integration, not network-wide financial realization. Vendor-selected success case; no representative sector average or proof all STLA lanes have equivalent opportunity.

[DHL/Vitesco, 10 April 2024](https://group.dhl.com/en/media-relations/press-releases/2024/vitesco-technologies-and-dhl-enter-into-strategic-partnership.html): centralized management of 12 European sites, about 100,000 movements and one-third of freight volume; consolidated deliveries and carrier procurement. Pilot savings were reported without a percentage. Independent corroboration of the mechanism, not of a 17% rate.

The EUR255m remains an aggressive conditional case. No newly invented haircut replaces it. Evidence can support route consolidation and better loading; it cannot establish 17% across inbound, outbound and warehousing combined. Do not apply the freight rate to the separate EUR1bn IT/services/other-indirect estimate.

## Warranty

Fewer early defects plausibly reduce future repairs, but 3MIS measures early claims frequency rather than lifetime cost. Repair severity, older vehicles, recalls, coverage and supplier recoveries also matter. Better expected lifetime claims can lower accrual expense on newly sold vehicles before the old fleet rolls off; cash payments follow a different schedule.

[Ford quality update, 16 July 2025](https://www.fromtheroad.ford.com/us/en/articles/2025/ford-quality-update), describes improving initial quality alongside more recalls from increased testing, including older vehicles. [Ford 2025 warranty rollforward](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/37996/000003799626000015/R134.htm) reports new-warranty accruals USD6,707m vs USD6,294m in 2024, and pre-existing revisions USD2,266m vs USD2,690m. Their sum was USD8,973m vs USD8,984m, approximately flat. This is not a controlled per-vehicle comparison or evidence quality improvement has no benefit; it demonstrates why operational quality improvement cannot be mapped one-for-one into total expense.

Our EUR3bn reference includes retained actuarial remeasurement and is not a clean recurring cohort expense base. Nonrecurrence of exceptional reserve charges is not operational VCP. Leave warranty savings unestimated and uncredited until a defensible claims-cost mapping exists; do not revive the rejected 5–10% assumption.

## Decision

No numerical revision. Manufacturing is defensible as a conditional scenario; logistics has materially weaker whole-pool support; warranty remains an open quantified forecast. Current EUR3.986bn subtotal includes EUR3.150bn components, EUR581m manufacturing and EUR255m logistics. It is a generous benchmark-transfer scenario, not independently validated net AOI improvement or a lower bound. Inflation, execution costs, pricing and overlap must still be reconciled separately.
