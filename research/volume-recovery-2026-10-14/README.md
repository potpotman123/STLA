# North American volume recovery: selective, conditional, and currently Ram-led

Research cutoff: **14 October 2026**. This package proposes assumptions; it does not change the forecast workbook, independent AI baseline, or model architecture.

**Position:** a partial volume recovery is defensible, but a broad household-demand recovery is not yet established. Ram light-duty provides the clearest current evidence. Grand Cherokee supply normalization and a commercially viable Cherokee rollout are the largest additional Jeep opportunities. Dealer repair helps execution, but high stock and incomplete dealer-profit evidence prevent treating it as an independently measurable volume uplift. Old discontinued sales and pre-pandemic peaks should not automatically return.

**Clarification after the user's management-comparison challenge:** this is not a demonstrated bearish volume forecast versus management. Our covered-US-family base implies 21.0% growth over 2025–28 (6.6% annually), versus management's displayed NA American-brand endpoints implying 34.5% over 2025–30 (6.1% annually). Geography, coverage and horizon differ. Also, ex-Jeep US sales grew 13.9% YTD; excluding both Jeep and Ram LD they grew 2.3%. The ex-Ram-LD decline below is real but should not imply generalized non-Ram weakness. Management itself assumes a flat industry and growth from product/segment expansion. Uncertainty about timing, conquest and profitable conversion does not establish that the strategy fails. [Arithmetic, scope and revised interpretation](management-comparison-correction.json). No forecast values changed.

## Testing the evidence map

**Supply and launch problems: supported for specific periods, not a universal explanation.** Management's latest preliminary release attributes roughly 25k fewer Q3 NA shipments each to Grand Cherokee and Compass shutdowns, while Ram added roughly 17k. That is a dated operational explanation for wholesale disruption. It does not establish that all lost customer sales were supply-constrained or will return. The test is post-shutdown retail sell-through at sustainable payments and stock levels. General semiconductor shortages and later refresh/trim/engine transitions must remain separate. [Q3 shipment release](https://www.stellantis.com/en/news/press-releases/2026/october/stellantis-reports-q3-2026-estimated-consolidated-shipments-of-1-3-million-units-down-1-percent-year-over-year)

**Pricing and product choices: plausible and operationally specific, but no estimated elasticity.** The Q2 call describes deliberately constrained Cherokee trims/channels because of economics, with more competitive trims planned for H1 2027. A website starting price or open order book is not evidence of broad dealer availability or profitable conversion. A volume assumption that depends on cheaper effective payments must carry its price/support cost into the revenue and AOI assumptions. [Q2 call, pp12–13](https://www.stellantis.com/content/dam/stellantis-corporate/investors/events-and-presentations/other-documents/Stellantis-Q2-2026-Financial-Results-Call-Transcript.pdf)

**Dealer repair: credible, incomplete.** Asbury's 2025 improvement in orderable options and Stellantis units supports better execution; Lithia's continued weakness and dealer bonus complaints contradict uniform repair. Current primary Cox evidence reports August days supply of Chrysler 104, Ram 127, Dodge 133, and Jeep at least twice industry 73. These include pipeline/in-transit and are not vehicle age. This is a constraint on further stocking, not a direct model-level demand estimate. Asbury's Q2 2026 CDJR revenue-mix fall from 9% to 6% is excluded from demand calibration because portfolio and denominator effects are unresolved. [Dealer evidence and exact locators](dealer-demand-check.md), [Cox inventory](https://www.coxautoinc.com/insights/august-2026-new-vehicle-inventory/)

**Underlying demand: heterogeneous before and after the pandemic.** In 2019 Cherokee, Compass and Renegade declined, while Ram pickups and Grand Cherokee grew. In the reused 2023–24 peer panel, Ram fell 16.1% while its selected competitor basket grew 4.4%; Grand Cherokee fell 11.6% while its basket fell 3.5%; Compass grew 16.1% versus peers' 9.3%. The normalization shock mattered, but no single company-wide weak-demand mechanism explains these differences. Selected baskets are not the whole market or matched household cohorts. Existing shopping, forum and listing proxies support hypotheses, not elasticities; another broad sentiment search would not resolve the current forecast's biggest uncertainties. [2019 company table](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/fca-us-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2019-sales-300980866.html), [frozen peer evidence](support/historical-peer-comparison.json)

**New products: separate verified milestones from unit assumptions.** ProMaster City has planned Q1 2027 dealer arrival; Cherokee has current deliveries and planned more competitive trims. Ram Dakota/compact products have 2028 production plans but insufficient customer-launch timing or mature volume evidence. New Chrysler crossover dates are insufficiently specified. These later launches remain unallocated. HEMI, Trailhawk and Durango derivatives are inside their family forecasts, never additional full-model sales. [Launch audit](launch-check.md)

**Customer sales and shipments: different forecasts.** Q2 NA consolidated shipments grew 37.8% versus combined sales growth of 5.8%; management described US stock rebuilding for launches and shutdowns. The populations differ, so their difference is not an exact inventory roll-forward. Q3 preliminary NA shipments subsequently fell to 357k from 404k. The H1 rebound cannot be extrapolated as household demand. Forecast regional wholesale separately, constrained by customer sales and dated regional stock observations. [Dealer/shipments reconciliation](dealer-demand-check.md)

## Provisional numerical position

The [annual scenarios](proposed-volume-inputs.md) cover **13 families representing 97.7% of 2025 US sales and 99.6% of YTD 2026 US sales**. Their provisional base subtotal is **1.408m in 2027 and 1.491m in 2028**. These are US retail-plus-fleet customer volumes, not complete US/NA totals or wholesale forecasts. Future omitted launches can become material.

For perspective, those same families sold 1.232m in 2025. A transparent **hypothetical** 2026 reference—YTD actual plus Q3 repeated in Q4—is 1.278m. The base adds 213k by 2028 versus that reference, or 16.7%. This reference is not an approved 2026 forecast or a seasonal adjustment.

| Main incremental dependency | 2028 base units | Increase versus 2026 reference | Mechanism and qualification |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Cherokee | 80k | +48.7k | Broader competitive trims; requires about 6,667 sales/month versus Q3's 3,448. Judgment, not an order-backed target. |
| Ram LD | 320k | +41.2k | Hold approximately Q3 annualized pace of 307k, with modest further growth. Retail and stock must confirm it. |
| Grand Cherokee | 215k | +37.2k | Partial return near 2024–25 sales as availability improves; persistent demand weakness would break this assumption. |
| ProMaster City | 20k | +20.0k | Q1 2027 arrival then ramp; least secure included input, with no confirmed order conversion. |
| Charger family | 30k | +17.5k | Broader ICE availability produces net family growth beyond BEV substitution. |

About **40% of the modeled incremental growth** comes from explicitly conditional Cherokee, Charger and ProMaster City ramps. Remaining growth is also conditional, especially Grand Cherokee. Scenario bounds are rounded analyst judgments rather than calibrated probabilities. The 2028 covered-family downside/base/upside is 1.142m / 1.491m / 1.760m; this wide spread expresses unresolved execution and demand outcomes, not statistical confidence.

No mechanical quarterly curve is supplied: annual cases are supportable as transparent judgments, but launch months, seasonality and latest classification reconciliation do not support precise quarterly allocations yet. Canada and Mexico remain simple flat placeholders against verified FY2025 country totals, with no detailed country model schedules.

## Strongest competing explanation and falsification

The alternative is that the visible recovery largely reflects weak comparisons, fleet/stock effects and financially supported clearance, while customers remain unconvinced by the offer. Ram's reported retail growth argues against explaining everything as wholesale stocking, but it does not prove durable share gains at stable net realization.

The partial-recovery thesis weakens if desirable products become widely available yet retail share remains weak, fresh inventory ages, or the effective payment concessions required to sell them keep increasing. It strengthens if two or three replenishment cycles show repeat retail conversion, dealer reorders and sound gross profit after earned incentives and holding costs.

## Three gaps that matter most

1. **Jeep conversion after supply and trim repair:** Grand Cherokee/Compass post-shutdown retail and stock, plus Cherokee's H1 2027 competitive-trim delivery and effective payments. Together these move more of the forecast than another generic brand-sentiment study.
2. **Ram sustainability and dealer economics:** comparable post-relaunch retail/fleet shares, fresh-stock turns and actual earned support. Best targeted transcript: **Asbury Q2 2026, 28 July call**, asking whether the earlier CDJR same-store improvement persisted and how stock, gross profit and portfolio changes explain it. One repeat CDJR operator interview with dated reorders and bonus economics would be more useful than many broad dealer calls.
3. **2028 launch timing and net addition:** customer availability and credible order scale for Dakota/compact Ram; substitution from 1500, Gladiator and other group products. Until these exist, do not insert mature full-year launch volumes. Exact dealer orders/receipts are more decisive than another promotional presentation.
