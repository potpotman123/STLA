# Dealer repair, replenishment and household demand

As of 14 October 2026. Decision: whether evidence supports an enduring, profitable North American volume recovery beyond the rebound in shipments.

**Judgment:** there is credible evidence of better dealer engagement and more desirable ordering options, followed by some brand-specific retail improvement. There is not yet evidence sufficient to size a national recovery caused by dealer repair, or to establish profitable replenishment. The strongest current counterweight is high stock relative to sales after shipments substantially outgrew sales. Price, financing support, model availability and dealer economics must be modeled jointly with volume.

The existing household audit also rejects a blanket permanent brand-rejection thesis: Q2 2025 company-reported Ram 1500 retail growth (+68%) materially exceeded total growth (+17%), while company-wide US total sales still fell 10%. This is supply/offer-sensitive improvement from a weak base, not a demand-elasticity estimate. Earlier group average transaction prices and shopping consideration are descriptive, mix-sensitive evidence only. [Existing household audit and original-source links](../../../outputs/household-demand-relative-value-audit.json)

**Model admission:** retain reported sales, shipments, dated stock and net-price bridges as historical inputs with their actual populations. Dealer testimony supports mechanisms and scenario triggers. No new national volume forecast, replenishment assumption, elasticity or dealer profit uplift is justified by this pass. Require at least two or three replenishment cycles in a matched model/trim population: actual receipts, retail deliveries, fresh versus aged stock, effective payments and dealer margin including earned support and holding cost. Fleet, transfers and launch effects must remain separate.

**Only decisive additional transcript requests:** (1) Asbury's **28 July 2026 Q2 call**, limited to CDJR same-store units, stock/age, gross profit, order acceptance and effects of portfolio changes, to establish whether the 2025 within-operator improvement persisted. (2) One **Q3 2026 repeat interview with an actual CDJR ordering/financial decision-maker**, preferably the same earlier operator, only if it covers dated receipts/reorders, fresh-stock retail turns and earned bonus economics. Generic optimism or a new all-brand dealer call will not resolve the question. Exact current incentive bulletins and stock/order ledgers would be more decisive than further opinion transcripts; no expensive collection authorized here.
