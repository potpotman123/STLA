# D090 — Volume-recovery evidence and provisional inputs

Recorded 14 October 2026. Prior VCP decisions, benchmark changes and independent AI baseline are preserved.

**User direction.** Shift from cost savings to volume; test whether unusual supply/launch errors and dealer friction are reversing, while treating earlier demand weakness as a potential limit to the recovery. Cover important models before deepening the largest uncertainties. The user asked for broad source discovery, competing mechanisms, numerical assumptions where supportable, and durable records. Prior corrections emphasized retail/fleet distinctions without paralysis, root causes rather than repeated commentary, model-specific histories, and avoiding unsupported precision.

**Starting expectation.** Temporary constraints and product gaps could support recovery. Existing positive dealer testimony was encouraging. Neither implied that the previous volume peak was sustainable or all lost customers would return.

**New evidence that changed the view.** The official Q3 table shows that Ram LD's YTD gain exceeds the entire company's US gain. Excluding LD, US sales fell 3.6%. Cherokee additions have not offset combined Compass/Grand Cherokee losses. August stock relative to sales remains high. These observations narrow the thesis to selective recovery, while the current shipment shutdown explanation keeps an availability mechanism plausible for Jeep.

**AI corrections during the work.** Earlier limitations about Charger powertrain visibility are superseded by the latest disclosed ICE/BEV split. ICE percentage growth alone exaggerates combined-family growth. Apparent Pacifica growth is partly a reporting-comparability issue; combining Voyager is supported by two arithmetic reconciliations, although the formal classification explanation remains unverified. H1 and quarterly totals differ by 159; neither vintage is silently overwritten. Missing current rows are not assigned zero. The 2023 pickup decline remains unallocated between LD and HD because the correct national split is absent.

**Analytical choice.** Use recent family sales rates and partial historical recoveries for continuing products. Do not select a single uniform company growth rate, restore 2019 mechanically, or regress sales against endogenous incentives. Segment-share methods would be preferable with a complete matched current denominator and credible pricing/availability controls, but the accessible evidence is stronger for observed family pace. Small rounded scenarios expose judgment more honestly than a finely fitted launch curve.

**What the numerical proposal does and does not establish.** The covered-family base is 1.408m US customer sales in 2027 and 1.491m in 2028. This is an initial analyst scenario for 13 families, not a user-approved forecast. About 40% of its growth versus a hypothetical flat-Q4 2026 reference requires conditional Cherokee, Charger and ProMaster City ramps. There is no statistically calibrated confidence interval, measured cannibalization coefficient or quantified dealer-repair uplift. New 2028 Ram/Chrysler launches remain unallocated. Canada/Mexico are explicitly stale-baseline flat placeholders, not precise country forecasts.

**Evidence admission.** Sales counts, scope definitions and dated stock observations can enter history with grouping checks. Operator testimony informs mechanisms and triggers, not national incremental-unit coefficients. Conditional launch assumptions need explicit judgment before adoption. No forecast workbook, independent baseline, model architecture, cost-savings assumption or revenue/AOI bridge was changed.

**Work performed and efficiency.** Reused prior audits, retrieved five small official sales tables, checked the current shipment release and country reporting, and assigned two narrow dealer/launch checks. One small existing-evidence follow-up assembled qualitative family rows. No bulk scraping, OCR, paid-data collection, mass PDF parsing or large model fitting. Failed/limited leads were parked: no exact original incentive program economics; no fresh national retail/fleet/price/stock panel; no precise 2028 customer-launch timing; Cox downloadable data and one dealer-results page were unavailable but directly inspectable primary evidence supported the narrower claims.

**Validation versus calibration.** The reproducible script checks group totals, published-vintage residuals, growth contributions and scenario ordering. Historical/current sales anchor the judgment, but do not independently validate its future outcome. Causal interpretation and forecast accuracy remain testable hypotheses.

**Next decisive observations.** Jeep retail conversion after availability improves; repeat dealer replenishment with earned margin and holding costs; and customer-launch dates/order conversion for the unallocated 2028 products. A targeted Asbury Q2 2026 call and one repeat dealer interview with operating data could resolve more than another broad transcript bundle.
