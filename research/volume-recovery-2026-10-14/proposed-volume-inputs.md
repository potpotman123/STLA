The quantified families represent **97.7% of 2025 US sales** and **99.6% of YTD September 2026 US sales**. This is not a complete 2028 US forecast: future unallocated launches can become material even if current omitted models are small.

**Reference convention:** 2026 YTD actual plus Q3 repeated as a hypothetical Q4. This is a transparent comparison device, not a claim about seasonality or an approved 2026 forecast. It prevents giving the 2027–28 recovery credit for gains already achieved by 2026.

**Scenario logic:** downside means stalled availability repair, persistent payment pressure and weak launches; base means current successful families hold their pace and selected gaps partly close; upside needs sustained retail conversion, broader attractive supply and no offsetting inventory build. The endpoints are rounded judgments, not evidence-backed causal coefficients.

**Ram light-duty pickups — Recent pace.** Q3 76,650 x4 = 306,600; YTD annualized 269,529. Base holds the better Q3 pace, then adds only 3% in 2028. Downside loses part of the ramp; upside needs sustained retail growth and mix availability. No separate HEMI or dealer-bonus additive uplift.

**Ram heavy-duty pickups — Partial normalization.** 2025 169,920; Q3 annualized 164,428; 2024 186,107. Base modestly exceeds the 2025 level and stays below 2024 through 2028. Recovery from the 2025 transition is not already proved by a weak Q3.

**Ram ProMaster — Recent pace.** YTD annualized 58,707; Q3 annualized 65,256; 2025 57,591. Base lies inside current annualized paces; old 2023 81,663 is not mechanically restored. Commercial fleet demand matters.

**Jeep Grand Cherokee — Partial normalization.** 2025 210,082; 2024 216,148; current YTD annualized 186,352 and Q3 annualized 152,108. Base assumes planned shutdown/availability effects ease, returning near 2025–24 rather than the 2023 peak. Largest continuing-Jeep supply-versus-demand risk.

**Jeep Wrangler — Recent pace.** YTD annualized 174,681; Q3 annualized 179,028; 2025 167,322. Base holds about current pace for both years; no restoration of 2019 228,032. Bronco and aging/mix competition cap assumed gains.

**Jeep Compass — Constrained partial recovery.** YTD annualized 63,783; Q3 annualized 43,244; 2025 101,997. Base starts near the YTD pace, with only limited 2028 recovery. Cherokee expansion and overlapping customers prevent simultaneous full restoration of both nameplates.

**Jeep Gladiator — Recent pace.** YTD annualized 41,259; Q3 annualized 47,644; 2025 56,790. Base lies within current paces; 2025 rebound is not a reliable full-year new baseline after renewed 2026 decline.

**Jeep Wagoneer / Grand Wagoneer — Recent pace.** YTD annualized 56,856; Q3 annualized 49,980; 2025 combined 45,040. Base broadly holds YTD scale with small additional growth. Label consolidation and high-price demand/incentives remain risks.

**Jeep Cherokee / returning HEV — Conditional launch judgment.** Q3 HEV 10,344 = 3,448/month; base requires 5,000/month in 2027 and 6,667 in 2028. H1 2027 competitive trims are a management plan, not measured conversion. Base is conditional on broader profitable availability. Not an independently calibrated launch curve; never restore old 191k peak automatically.

**Dodge Charger / Challenger transition family — Conditional launch judgment.** Q3 new ICE+BEV 3,679 = 1,226/month. Base requires about 1,667/month then 2,500/month, supported directionally by ICE rollout but not by order data. Gasoline growth currently offsets BEV decline; old Charger+Challenger 120,858 in 2023 is not assumed recoverable.

**Dodge Durango — Recent pace.** 2025 81,168; YTD annualized 81,773; Q3 annualized 91,020. Base is near current scale in both years. New V8 derivatives sit inside this family, not additive launch forecasts; age and price constrain sustained gains.

**Chrysler Pacifica / Voyager — Recent pace.** 2025 Pacifica+Voyager 125,798; current YTD annualized 135,481 and Q3 annualized 137,964. Base stays close to current pace, then grows modestly. This includes fleet and depends on the apparent Voyager reporting consolidation.

**Ram ProMaster City — Conditional launch judgment.** Dealer arrival is planned Q1 2027; old 2019 nameplate sold 12,920. Base requires 833/month then 1,667/month. Different product/use and launch risks make old sales a scale check, not a causal calibration. This is the least secure included base input.

**Numerically unallocated:** Dakota/compact Ram launches, new Chrysler crossovers, Recon, Wagoneer S, Hornet and small-brand/runoff volumes. No assumed zero economic potential; evidence is insufficient to size a recovery. The launch register records the gates.

**Country convention:** Canada 115k and Mexico 91k held flat in both years as explicit placeholders from the FY2025 reported rounded country baseline. Replace with the latest validated 2026 country exit before admitting them as forecast inputs. No separate detailed country schedules.

**Quarterly application:** this pass supports annual scenarios, not a defensible twelve-quarter demand curve. Current-product seasonality, quarter-specific availability and launch dates must be reconciled before allocating annual units. A 2028 production start cannot justify four quarters of mature customer sales.

**Portfolio check:** base 2028 Cherokee + Compass + Grand Cherokee = 365k, versus 312.6k in 2025 and 330.7k in 2024. Cherokee expansion is paired with Compass below its previous level; no assumption that all three fully recover independently. This is a portfolio constraint, not measured cannibalization. Base new-model gross sales and legacy replacement effects must not be counted a second time in a launch uplift row.

**Model admission:** observed sales can populate history after the stated grouping/reconciliation checks. Current-pace scenarios are usable as provisional assumptions. Conditional launch scenarios require explicit user judgment; unallocated launches and dealer economics remain gates. Do not multiply these US customer volumes directly by revenue per wholesale shipment.
